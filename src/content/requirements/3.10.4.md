## Requirement

"Maintain audit logs of physical access."

## Objectives

[a] audit logs of physical access are maintained.

## Sufficient

- **Either a procedural log (a paper sign-in sheet with name and date) or an automated log (a badge reader or PIV capture), or a combination of both.** [a]
- **Motion-activated camera footage where every clip carries its own timestamp.** Each timestamped clip is treated as a log entry in its own right. [a]
- **An alarm system's arm-and-disarm event history, paired with a paper visitor log.** [a]
- **An ERP or job-tracking system's scan-in and scan-out records, corroborated with camera footage**, accepted where badge readers are not installed at every entrance. [a]
- **Entry-only logging, with no matching sign-out event captured.** Most badge systems do not force a logged exit either, so entry alone has been treated as the same concept. [a]
- **A login record tied to a named location matching an office's static IP address**, used as corroborating evidence of physical presence. [a]
- **A solo home-office operator who holds every key logging the simple fact that no one else was present on a given day**, with more detailed time entries reserved for days a visitor is present, as long as the record explains how access by anyone else is ruled out. [a]
- **An organization running entirely on cloud infrastructure, with no on-premises equipment, inheriting this requirement from its cloud provider**, while still separately addressing logging for any alternate work sites its people use. [a]

## Insufficient

- **A time clock used as the sole access log, when employees are not required to clock out for short breaks, when multiple entrances sit far from the clock, or when salaried staff are not required to use it at all.** These gaps mean the clock alone does not add up to a complete record of who accessed the facility. [a]
- **Treating a computer's login history as a physical access log.** A laptop login record does not stand in for a record of who physically entered a facility or controlled area. [a]

## Over-engineering

- **AI-driven facial recognition or clothing-pattern-matching camera systems.** A timestamped camera clip or a badge reader already satisfies the objective at a fraction of the cost and complexity. [a]
- **Regularly reviewing the logs as a standing practice.** The objective is to maintain them so they are available if an incident requires investigation; they do not need to be more searchable than papers kept in a folder, and there is no requirement to review them on a schedule. [a]
- **Layering in additional automated visitor-record maintenance and review controls drawn from the broader 800-53 catalog.** Those controls sit outside what this specific requirement, and the CMMC assessment against it, evaluates. [a]

## Edge cases

- **A home office that also functions as the organization's primary facility**, with servers or other equipment present, still needs physical access logs the same as any office. A locked room plus a locked equipment cabinet is a reasonable layered baseline, and the logging itself can be simplified when one person controls every key and is the sole regular occupant.
- **Multiple building entrances with no badge reader at every door** can be covered with camera footage at each entrance, corroborated against another system's records, such as an ERP job-tracking log, rather than requiring a reader at every entrance.
- **External consumer cameras, such as doorbell or outdoor security cameras, used for exterior entrance monitoring**, are acceptable as long as they are not pointed at an interior space where CUI is visible; connecting them over a guest network rather than the CUI network is an accepted way to keep them out of scope.

## Evidence examples

- **A paper visitor logbook capturing name and date and time of entry.** [a]
- **Badge or PIV reader access logs.** [a]
- **Motion-activated camera footage where each clip carries a timestamp.** [a]
- **An alarm system's arm-and-disarm event history.** [a]
- **A named-location login audit trail correlated to an office's static IP address.** [a]
- **ERP or job-tracking scan records used to corroborate physical presence.** [a]

## Related requirements

- **3.10.1** defines who is authorized to be present; this requirement records that access as it actually happens.
- **3.10.3** produces the visitor sign-in and escort record that partly satisfies this requirement's log for non-employee entries.
- **3.10.6** governs the alternate-work-site case, including a home office used as a primary facility, that shapes how this requirement's logging gets simplified outside a traditional office.
