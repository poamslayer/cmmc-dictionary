## Requirement

"Authenticate (or verify) the identities of those users, processes, or devices, as a prerequisite to allowing access to organizational information systems."

## Objectives

[a] the identity of each user is authenticated or verified as a prerequisite to system access;
[b] the identity of each process acting on behalf of a user is authenticated or verified as a prerequisite to system access; and
[c] the identity of each device accessing or connecting to the system is authenticated or verified as a prerequisite to system access.

## Sufficient

- **A physical inventory of devices with a fixed, known location, in place of a technical device-authentication mechanism.** The requirement allows authentication or verification, so a small organization that can reliably account for a stable set of devices in a controlled area can use that inventory as its verification step. [c]
- **Remote devices joined to the directory or enrolled in mobile device management.** These already attest device identity during the sign-in process, satisfying device authentication without a separate mechanism layered on top. [c]
- **A documented list of allowed MAC address and IP address pairings**, used as the verification record when a technical device-authentication mechanism is not otherwise in place. Assessors view this favorably because it ties a specific address combination to a specific approved device rather than leaving the claim unverified. [c]

## Insufficient

- **Leaving factory default usernames and passwords active on a device or system.** Default credentials are widely known and easily discovered, so they do not verify a genuine identity claim. [a][c]
- **Assuming a cloud-only environment has no endpoints that need device or user authentication.** CUI still moves down to endpoints for editing and back up when work products are created, and prints pass through a physical device; those endpoints remain in scope for authentication. [a][c]

## Over-engineering

- **Building a full technical device-authentication mechanism for a specialized asset that cannot support one.** Assets of this kind are documented with a risk-based alternative approach instead of being forced into a mechanism their hardware or software cannot run.

## Edge cases

- **Remote workers on home networks raise the same authentication expectation as an office network.** Which objective actually applies depends on how the endpoint was categorized during scoping; most endpoints that touch CUI carry every applicable requirement regardless of where they physically sit.
- **Specialized assets that cannot technically support device authentication are one of the few asset types where this specific objective may not be achievable as designed.** Documenting the asset and, where practical, isolating it from the rest of the environment is the accepted alternative rather than forcing a mechanism the asset cannot run.
- **An alternate work site protection plan is the expected companion documentation for remote endpoints**, addressing how user and device authentication carries over outside a controlled office.

## Evidence examples

- **Directory join record or mobile device management enrollment confirmation.** [c]
- **Documented MAC address and IP address pairing list** for devices without a technical authentication mechanism. [c]
- **Physical inventory list** for devices in a controlled, fixed location. [c]
- **Asset categorization documentation** showing why device authentication does or does not apply to a given endpoint. [c]
- **Alternate work site protection plan** covering remote endpoint authentication. [a][c]

## Related requirements

- **3.5.1** identifies the users, processes, and devices this requirement then authenticates; identification names them, and this requirement verifies the claim.
- **3.5.3** adds a second factor on top of the single-factor verification this requirement establishes for privileged and non-privileged network access.
- **3.1.12** through **3.1.15** govern remote access sessions that depend on the device and user authentication this requirement establishes as a precondition.
