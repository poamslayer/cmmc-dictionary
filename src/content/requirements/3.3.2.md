## Requirement

"Ensure that the actions of individual system users can be uniquely traced to those users so they can be held accountable for their actions."

## Objectives

[a] the content of the audit records needed to support the ability to uniquely trace users to their actions is defined; and
[b] audit records, once created, contain the defined content.

## Sufficient

- **A defined set of standard audit-record content elements, such as what happened, when it happened, where it happened, what triggered it, the outcome, and who was involved**, used as the baseline for what the organization needs to trace a user to an action. Whether this baseline is sufficient depends on how the underlying system is actually configured to collect it, not just on having the definition written down. [a]
- **The organization sets its own bar for what content is needed to reach unique traceability.** Practitioner consensus treats this as squarely within the organization's own definitional authority once it is defined and then demonstrated. [a]

## Insufficient

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Over-engineering

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Edge cases

- **A written policy that references a standard content baseline without confirming the audit system is actually configured to collect it.** Practitioner consensus treats the policy alone as unproven; showing how the system is configured to capture that content, and pulling a live sample that shows it actually is captured, is the way to close the gap between the paper definition and the running system. [a][b]

## Evidence examples

- **A configuration export from the logging platform showing that per-user identity fields are turned on for capture.** [a]
- **A sample of audit records demonstrating the defined content is actually present at the collected level of detail.** [b]
- **Documentation defining the content needed for unique user traceability**, which may reference a recognized standard content baseline. [a]

## Related requirements

- **3.3.1** is the broader logging requirement this one narrows: 3.3.1 defines and retains audit records generally, and this requirement checks that those records specifically carry enough content to trace a user to their action.
- **3.1.7**, control of non-privileged users' use of privileged functions, depends on the traceability this requirement establishes to be checkable at all.
- **3.5.1** and **3.5.2**, identification and authentication, supply the unique identity this requirement's traceability is built on.
