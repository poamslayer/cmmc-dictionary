## Requirement

"Mark media with necessary CUI markings and distribution limitations."

## Objectives

[a] media containing CUI is marked with applicable CUI markings; and
[b] media containing CUI is marked with distribution limitations.

## Sufficient

- **A training policy that instructs every user to mark CUI-bearing media, combined with physical labels IT applies to issued external media identifying it as containing controlled data.** Accepted in practice, including at organizations that have passed a government assessment on this basis. [a][b]
- **Limiting the marking obligation to removable and portable media rather than fixed drives, workstations, or laptops.** The Assessment Guide's worked examples for the 3.8 requirements name paper, microfilm, optical discs, USB drives, and external or removable drives, and do not extend to fixed internal storage. [a]
- **Minimal marking on space-limited media**, per federal marking guidance: the general CUI control marking ("CONTROLLED" or "CUI") plus the designating agency, when a device is too small to carry full category, subcategory, or dissemination-control markings. [a][b]
- **Not marking computers located inside a controlled area**, consistent with the allowance in the underlying source security control; a controlled area can rest on physical barriers (a locked room or storage container) or on procedural controls such as direct physical possession. [a]
- **Exempting non-portable media types from the labeling obligation once they sit inside a controlled environment**, while continuing to label removable and portable devices regardless of location, consistent with federally assigned marking parameters for CUI systems. [a]
- **Marking paper media the same way as digital media.** Assessors generally do not scrutinize paper marking closely in practice, but the marking obligation still applies to it. [a]
- **Carrying forward a customer's existing markings on derivative paper documents rather than generating new ones.** A subcontractor is almost never the designating agency for CUI it receives, so the lower-cost, compliant path is to keep the customer's banner and distribution statement intact and state the carry-forward rule in policy; an incoming DoD distribution statement already on a drawing satisfies the distribution-limitation objective without a new marking process. [a][b]
- **Policy language that marks documents "when required by contract" rather than unconditionally.** A contractor is an authorized holder of CUI, not the designating agency that determines information qualifies as CUI in the first place; for material the contractor itself produces, a CUI marking is warranted only when the governing contract calls for it, which makes conditional policy wording accurate rather than a gap. [a]

## Insufficient

- **Relying on a CUI logon banner as the sole method of marking media.** Practitioner consensus is skeptical of a logon banner standing in for physical or file-level marking of the media itself. [a]
- **Treating FIPS encryption on removable media as a substitute for marking it.** An encrypted device containing CUI still needs a physical marking, because the current DoD position continues to treat encrypted CUI as CUI regardless of encryption status. [a]
- **Leaving removable or portable media unmarked inside a controlled environment.** Federally assigned marking parameters still require marking on removable and portable storage even when it never leaves a controlled space. [a]

## Over-engineering

- **Applying CUI labels to every workstation and laptop.** Many assessors do not require labels on fixed workstations, and a visible label is widely viewed as creating a target rather than reducing risk; large defense contractors have passed government assessments without laptop labeling. [a]
- **Applying every possible CUI category, subcategory, and dissemination-control marking to small media.** Federal marking guidance explicitly allows minimal marking, just the general control marking and designating agency, when a device's size does not permit more. [a][b]
- **Building an automatic CUI banner into a standard business document template so every derivative document is marked by default.** That practice puts the contractor in the position of designating CUI on its own output with no contract instruction behind it; where the source document already carries its own markings, a wrapper built around it needs no additional banner of its own. [a]
- **Extending media marking requirements to fixed internal drives.** The underlying standard leaves ongoing protection of fixed storage to dedicated encryption-at-rest controls instead; a common working rule is that a drive requiring a tool to remove is a fixed drive, not media within this requirement's scope. [a]

## Edge cases

- **Laptops that regularly leave a controlled space for remote or hybrid work.** Labeling appears warranted for a laptop routinely taken off-site, but procedural controls, direct possession, screen locking, and closing files around others, can establish a controlled-area concept at home too; an encrypted laptop that is powered off or locked exposes no CUI regardless of labeling. The outcome depends on the assessor.
- **Thin-client devices that transmit CUI without storing it locally**, for example over a video call. These may not need labeling at all, since the marking requirement targets media that stores CUI rather than devices that only display or transmit it.
- **Receiving unmarked material believed to be CUI from an upstream prime.** A downstream recipient without visibility into government ownership or contractual status of the material should not build an elaborate marking response around that uncertainty; that question is separate from, and often more consequential than, media marking itself.
- **Multiple CUI types stored on one small device.** The general control marking plus designating agency is sufficient; federal marking guidance does not require every applicable category and subcategory marking where the device lacks room for it.
- **Marking a shipping container versus marking the media inside it.** Federal CUI marking regulation is explicit that the outside of an envelope or package should not carry CUI markings or otherwise reveal that it contains CUI; the media itself is marked, the outer container is not. A transmittal document accompanying the shipment, however, must carry the general control marking along with instructions for what to do once the enclosure is opened.

## Evidence examples

- **CUI marking training policy** instructing users to label media containing CUI. [a][b]
- **Physical labels** on removable media such as USB drives, external drives, and optical discs. [a]
- **Asset tracking system** identifying which devices carry CUI. [a]
- **Controlled-area policy** defining where marking exemptions apply and why. [a]
- **Marked paper documents** carrying carried-forward customer distribution statements. [a][b]
- **Minimal device labels** reading "CONTROLLED" or "CUI" plus designating agency on space-limited media. [a][b]

## Related requirements

- **3.8.1** physically protects the same media this requirement marks.
- **3.8.5** tracks marked media for accountability once it leaves a controlled area for transport.
- **3.8.8** requires an identifiable owner for portable storage devices, which pairs naturally with a device label that also shows ownership.
