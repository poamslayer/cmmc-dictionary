## Requirement

"Remediate vulnerabilities in accordance with risk assessments."

## Objectives

[a] vulnerabilities are identified; and
[b] vulnerabilities are remediated in accordance with risk assessments.

## Sufficient

- **At least two worked examples that trace a vulnerability from discovery through fix**: identified in a scan, tracked as a ticket, remediated, then confirmed closed in a later scan. Assessment Guide discussion frames remediation as informed by risk, and a before-and-after pair is the clearest way to show that decision process in action. [a][b]
- **A written risk-acceptance standard describing which severity levels get remediated versus accepted**, for example remediating everything above a stated severity threshold while formally accepting lower-severity findings after review for false positives. [b]
- **A formal, documented exception for legacy or unsupported systems that cannot be patched**, stating the rationale and any compensating controls, rather than leaving the gap unexplained. [b]
- **A documented investigation showing a specific finding's exploit conditions are not present** in the environment, for example a required port or configuration that does not exist, followed by reclassifying that finding as informational rather than remediating it. [a][b]
- **Isolating unsupported embedded equipment behind compensating network controls**, such as a restricted network segment, no internet access, and access limited to a controlled jump point, when the equipment cannot be upgraded without breaking its function. [b]

## Insufficient

- **A spreadsheet of findings with no supporting policy explaining the remediation criteria behind it.** A list alone does not show how remediation decisions were made. [b]
- **Addressing only the highest-severity findings while leaving medium and lower findings untouched with no documented reasoning.** Every finding needs to be addressed in some way, even if the outcome for a lower-severity finding is a documented risk acceptance rather than a fix. [b]
- **Dismissing a finding as not applicable without investigating whether its exploit conditions actually exist in the environment.** A reclassification decision needs a documented basis, not an assumption. [a][b]

## Over-engineering

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Edge cases

- **Legacy software that cannot be upgraded without breaking required functionality.** The remediation decision is organizationally defined rather than absolute: if a fix is planned, a temporary deficiency approach fits; if no fix is planned, a written, isolated exception with rationale and compensating controls is the accepted path.
- **Specialized manufacturing equipment running an unsupported embedded operating system.** Where upgrading the OS would break the equipment, classifying it as a specialized asset with documented risk-based handling, network restriction, and no direct internet access is the accepted resolution rather than treating it as an ordinary CUI asset finding.
- **How this requirement differs from the related timing and tracking requirements around it.** This requirement covers the remediation decision itself; scanning that surfaces the finding, the plan-of-action process that tracks it, and the patching process that closes flaws generally are each handled by neighboring requirements, and organizations often manage all of them as one connected process rather than as separate silos.

## Evidence examples

- **Two full-lifecycle remediation examples**: an initial scan showing the finding, a tracking record, the remediation action, and a follow-up scan confirming closure. [a][b]
- **Written risk-acceptance criteria** stating which severities are remediated versus accepted, and how false positives are handled. [b]
- **Formal exception documentation for legacy or unsupported systems**, including compensating controls and asset classification. [b]
- **An investigation record showing a finding's exploit conditions were checked** before a reclassification decision. [a][b]
