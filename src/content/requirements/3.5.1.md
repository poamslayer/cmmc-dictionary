## Requirement

"Identify information system users, processes acting on behalf of users, or devices."

## Objectives

[a] system users are identified;
[b] processes acting on behalf of users are identified; and
[c] devices accessing the system are identified.

## Sufficient

- **A username, email address, or employee ID as the user identifier**, with unique identifiers managed through the identity directory. Assessors accept these as satisfying user identification when each one maps to one individual. [a]
- **Device identifiers assigned through a mobile device management platform, tied to the manufacturer's serial number.** This is accepted as a unique, non-reusable device identifier and also supports the identifier-reuse objective elsewhere in this family. [c]
- **MAC address tracking for devices outside a management platform**, or a network access control solution that registers devices before allowing them onto the network. [c]
- **An organization-defined identification method in policy or procedure for devices that fall outside any management platform.** Whatever method is chosen, documenting it is what assessors expect to see. [c]
- **Service accounts documented with a stated purpose**, for example an account named for the log-ingestion pipeline it supports. The test assessors apply is whether the account's function and business reason are written down, not whether the account is unusual. [b]
- **Shared or role-based accounts, including privileged accounts released through a password vault, accepted when the vault preserves a checkout record.** A log showing who retrieved the credential and when supplies the traceability that a shared account otherwise lacks. [a][b]

## Insufficient

- **A shared or privileged account with no vault, checkout log, or rotation mechanism behind it.** Without a record of who used the account and when, there is no way to trace activity back to an individual, and the identification objective is not met. [a]
- **Pointing only to the identity directory's account list without a separate service-account purpose statement.** A directory entry shows an account exists; it does not show what the account does or why it exists, which is what process identification asks for. [b]
- **Devices with no assigned identifier at all** because they sit outside both the management platform and any documented fallback method (no MAC record, no NAC registration, no policy-defined alternative). [c]

## Over-engineering

- **Individually documenting built-in operating-system service accounts** (the accounts an operating system creates for its own internal functions). A single line noting that these are native platform accounts required for the system to run is enough; itemizing each one adds no assurance.
- **Treating an application a user runs under their own login as a separate "process acting on behalf of a user."** A word processor or mail client running as the signed-in user is the user's own activity, not a distinct service identity, and documenting it as one satisfies no objective. [b]

## Edge cases

- **This requirement is often confused with the access-authorization requirement.** The two share nearly identical objective wording, but access authorization governs who is approved to hold an account, while this requirement governs how the identity infrastructure labels the resulting user, process, and device identities once they exist.
- **Deciding whether an integration falls under objective [b] comes down to one question: was a distinct account created for it to run under?** This covers an application-to-database connection, a scheduled task, or a cloud integration alike. A cloud service principal used this way is treated the same as an on-premises service account. [b]
- **A service account tied to infrastructure, such as the account a web server process runs under, belongs in the same inventory as any other service account**, even though it supports infrastructure rather than an application. [b]

## Evidence examples

- **Mobile device management inventory export** showing device identifiers tied to serial numbers. [c]
- **Documented policy defining the identification method** for devices outside the management platform, such as MAC address tracking. [c]
- **List of service accounts with a stated purpose for each.** [b]
- **Checkout log from a privileged password vault** showing which individual retrieved a shared credential and when. [a][b]
- **Identity directory export of user accounts.** [a]
- **Network access control device registry**, for organizations using that method for unmanaged devices. [c]

## Related requirements

- **3.1.1** authorizes who gets an account; this requirement is the technical labeling layer the identity infrastructure applies once that account exists.
- **3.5.2** authenticates the identities this requirement establishes; identification names the entity, authentication verifies the claim behind it.
- **3.5.5** governs reuse of the same device and user identifiers this requirement creates, and depends on those identifiers being unique in the first place.
