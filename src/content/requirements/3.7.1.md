## Requirement

"Perform maintenance on organizational systems."

## Objectives

[a] system maintenance is performed.

## Sufficient

- **For general-purpose endpoints, routine security patching together with responsiveness to user-reported issues has been accepted as adequate evidence that maintenance is being performed.** This requirement's own objective is worded broadly, and practitioner consensus does not read it as demanding more than ordinary patch and repair activity for typical office systems. [a]
- **For infrastructure hosted under a cloud shared-responsibility model, the provider's handling of its own infrastructure layer counts toward this requirement for the portion of the system it operates.** The organization still owns maintenance of what it controls above that layer. [a]

## Insufficient

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Over-engineering

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Edge cases

- **Because the objective's wording is broad, there is room for a wide range of interpretations of what counts as maintenance on a given system type.** Practitioner consensus treats ordinary patching and issue response as sufficient for typical endpoints without further elaboration, and treats a cloud provider's own maintenance of its infrastructure as covering that layer without the organization needing to separately document it.

## Evidence examples

- **Patch management records or update logs.** [a]
- **Help desk or ticketing records showing maintenance-related work performed.** [a]
- **Cloud service provider responsibility documentation identifying which maintenance layer the provider covers.** [a]

## Related requirements

- **3.7.2** governs the tools, techniques, mechanisms, and personnel used to carry out the maintenance this requirement calls for.
- **3.4.3** and **3.4.4** control how maintenance-driven changes to a system get tracked once they occur.
