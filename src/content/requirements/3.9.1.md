## Requirement

"Screen individuals prior to authorizing access to organizational systems containing CUI."

## Objectives

[a] individuals are screened prior to authorizing access to organizational systems containing CUI.

## Sufficient

- **An existing hiring policy, well documented and applied consistently going forward**, without standing up a separate CUI-specific screening track. Practitioner consensus treats a company's normal pre-employment screening as adequate once it is written down and followed. [a]
- **A well-established onboarding process with a CUI-specific step layered in**, where HR procedures are updated to flag which roles will reach CUI, and those individuals are provisioned accordingly once screened. An organization has passed a CMMC assessment on this approach. [a]
- **Screening scoped to the role and the data it touches**, rather than one uniform check for every hire. Assessment Guide discussion frames screening as evaluating an individual's trustworthiness against criteria set for the position and the access level required, which practitioner consensus reads as: heavier roles get heavier screening, and the rest can follow a baseline check. [a]
- **A documented screening program that leans on standard practice**, such as criminal background and credit checks, applied consistently before CUI access is granted and following applicable law and organizational policy for the position. [a]

## Insufficient

- **Granting CUI access before screening is complete**, or with no screening step in the hiring or role-change process at all. The objective's own wording, "prior to authorizing access," makes timing the test: screening that happens after access is already in place does not satisfy it. [a]
- **No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.**

## Over-engineering

- **Re-screening long-tenured employees under a new, heavier bar the moment they pick up CUI-related duties.** Practitioner consensus treats this as overreach: applying a documented policy going forward, rather than retroactively screening staff who have already been with the organization for many years, is treated as meeting the objective without redoing history. [a]

## Edge cases

- **Whether existing long-tenured employees need to be screened retroactively when they first gain CUI access.** One view is that this is fundamentally an HR policy question, turning on whether the organization already re-screens periodically or only screens at hire; the practitioner consensus answer is that a documented policy applied going forward is enough and no retroactive screening of veteran staff is expected.
- **How coverage of third parties, joint-venture partners, or subcontractors fits alongside this requirement.** The current text only reaches an organization's own individuals; screening obligations for outside personnel are addressed by separate requirements, and a later revision of the underlying NIST catalog is understood to expand explicit coverage of external personnel, information exchange, external services, and supply chain in that area, though this control file does not restate that later text.

## Evidence examples

- **Written personnel security or HR screening policy** naming the screening types tied to roles with CUI access. [a]
- **Pre-employment screening records**, such as background check or drug test completion, for personnel authorized to reach CUI. [a]
- **A list or role mapping identifying which positions carry CUI access**, produced as part of updated HR procedure. [a]
- **Dated records showing screening completed before the individual's CUI access was provisioned**, tying the screening event to the access grant. [a]

## Related requirements

- **3.9.2** picks up where this requirement ends, protecting systems during and after the personnel actions that follow screening and onboarding.
- **3.5.2** authenticates the identities this requirement has already vetted; the Assessment Guide notes that effective screening strengthens the value of that authentication step.
