## Requirement

"Provide privacy and security notices consistent with applicable CUI rules."

## Objectives

[a] privacy and security notices required by CUI-specified rules are identified, consistent, and associated with the specific CUI category; and
[b] privacy and security notices are displayed.

## Sufficient

- **A login banner presented before authentication, that a user must acknowledge or click through.** This has consistently been accepted in federal contractor assessments as sufficient display of the required notice. [b]
- **A desktop background carrying the notice, with no click-through requirement.** This has also been accepted where a click-through banner is not used; the notice still displays every time the desktop is visible. [b]
- **A single notice at the primary entry point**, rather than a separate notice at every downstream system or application a user might reach after logging in. Whether a secondary notice is needed for a particular downstream application is a risk-based decision, not a default requirement. [b]
- **Generic language indicating the system may contain CUI, without naming every specific CUI category the organization handles.** The CUI-specified categories generally only require an indication that CUI may be present, not an enumeration of every subcategory. [a]
- **Printed materials or posters in physical spaces where an automated banner is not practical** (a shared printer area, for example) are an accepted substitute for a digital notice in that specific location. [b]
- **A notice delivered during device enrollment in a mobile device management platform**, referencing the organization's CUI handling policy, has been accepted as satisfying the notice requirement for that device population. [b]

## Insufficient

- **Demanding a login notice on every single platform anywhere CUI might be stored, including consumer-style cloud storage.** This reading has been challenged as too broad; where access to those platforms already requires passing through a managed device with its own notice at initial logon, a second notice at every downstream platform is not treated as a hard requirement, though the answer depends on the assessor's read of how much risk the extra hop introduces.

## Over-engineering

- **Displaying a notice at every application hop after the initial network logon, regardless of risk.** The Assessment Guide's own framing calls for a risk-based decision about whether a secondary notice is needed, not an automatic requirement to notify at every layer.
- **Copying a government system's own standard warning banner language word for word for a private contractor system.** That language is written for government-owned systems and does not reference CUI at all, which is the actual basis for this requirement; a contractor-appropriate banner, reviewed by legal counsel, is the better fit.
- **Physically labeling every device with a CUI marking or sticker.** Marking equipment is optional under national CUI marking guidance, not mandated, and carries its own downside: a visible label can flag a device as worth targeting.
- **Posting CUI signage throughout every workspace.** This is not supported as a requirement; secure storage locations are the place physical marking is expected, not general work areas.
- **Building a persistent, always-visible banner overlay modeled on a government system's interface.** A standard pre-authentication login banner is the accepted level of implementation; an always-on-top banner is not required.

## Edge cases

- **Large organizations where only a small subset of staff ever touches CUI.** Three approaches have circulated: show a general notice about possible CUI access to everyone at login, restrict the notice to the specific enclave or application where CUI actually resides, or trigger a targeted popup only for the relevant subset of users. Each has tradeoffs, and organizations have pushed back on a universal banner as unnecessarily disruptive for staff who will never see CUI.
- **Whether a login-notice-per-platform reading is required, versus a single notice at the primary entry point being sufficient when downstream access is gated behind a managed device.** Practitioner opinion has largely landed on the single-entry-point reading as reasonable, but a stricter per-platform reading has also been applied in at least one review, so the outcome depends on the assessor. [b]
- **CUI-specified categories with their own notice rules.** The specified-category notice requirement in the CUI regulation has not, in practice, been implemented through category-specific Department of Defense guidance yet, so most organizations are working from the general CUI-basic notice expectation rather than category-specific rules. [a]
- **Mobile devices.** Notices on mobile devices are rarely implemented in practice; where an organization keeps mobile devices free of CUI entirely, the requirement does not apply to that device class.
- **Character limits in cloud login-banner features.** Some platforms cap banner text length well below what a full legal notice would need; shortened notice language, or delivering the full notice as an attached document through a separate access-control mechanism, are workarounds in use.

## Related requirements

- **3.8** (media protection) and physical CUI handling policies intersect with the printed-notice variant of this requirement.
- **3.1.1** and **3.5.2** define who reaches the login point where this notice is displayed.
- **32 CFR 2002** is the underlying regulation this requirement traces to; the CUI-specified notice objective only becomes concrete once a specific category's rules are published.
