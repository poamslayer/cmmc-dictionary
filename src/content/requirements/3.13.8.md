## Requirement

"Implement cryptographic mechanisms to prevent unauthorized disclosure of CUI during transmission unless otherwise protected by alternative physical safeguards."

## Objectives

[a] cryptographic mechanisms intended to prevent unauthorized disclosure of CUI are identified;
[b] alternative physical safeguards intended to prevent unauthorized disclosure of CUI are identified; and
[c] either cryptographic mechanisms or alternative physical safeguards are implemented to prevent unauthorized disclosure of CUI during transmission.

## Sufficient

- **A standard, dedicated common-carrier circuit relied on without additional encryption.** The Assessment Guide's discussion recognizes that a commodity telecommunication service can serve as the alternative physical safeguard when the necessary assurances are documented; a genuinely dedicated common-carrier circuit is accepted this way, while a software-defined network built to resemble one is not, because it lacks the same physical separation. [b][c]
- **Current-version TLS enforced with the underlying cryptographic module in FIPS mode**, satisfying transmission protection when paired with the FIPS-validation standard the related encryption requirement sets. [a][c]
- **A government-authorized cloud email or collaboration platform that only negotiates the current TLS version and relies on FIPS-validated cryptography under its own configuration**, satisfying this requirement for that traffic without additional organizational configuration. [a][c]
- **Mail flow connectors configured to enforce TLS for specific, verified partner domains**, rather than relying on default or best-effort negotiation that can silently fail. [c]
- **Documenting that a downstream government or prime-contractor system carries its own transmission-encryption responsibility**, with the organization's documented responsibility limited to its own end of that exchange. [a]

## Insufficient

- **A non-standard "MPLS" circuit built on a software-defined network, left unencrypted.** Even when a carrier markets the service as MPLS, a software-defined network does not carry the same dedicated separation as a true common-carrier circuit, and an assessment has deducted points for leaving such a circuit unencrypted. Assessor treatment of even standard MPLS varies; document the circuit's actual technical characteristics rather than relying on its name. [b][c]
- **Best-effort or opportunistic TLS on legacy email transport**, which silently downgrades to an older protocol or no encryption at all when the receiving system is misconfigured. Neither end can reliably verify the other enforced encryption, so this does not satisfy the objective on its own. [c]
- **Wireless access points transmitting CUI without FIPS-validated encryption.** Assessors generally do not accept a facility's physical security as a substitute for encrypting wireless traffic, even inside a controlled space, unless the signal itself cannot leave a fully secured area. [a][c]
- **Treating an unusual company profile or an obscure network path as a reason encryption is unnecessary.** This is not an accepted basis for skipping the requirement. [c]

## Over-engineering

- **Purchasing individual end-to-end message-level encryption certificates for every user when transport-layer TLS already enforces FIPS-validated encryption end to end.** Message-level encryption can add genuine benefits, such as user awareness of CUI handling and non-repudiation, but it is not required to satisfy this requirement once the transport layer already covers it.
- **Re-encrypting data a second time before handing it to a system that is already an approved, FIPS-encrypted transfer path.** The receiving system's own protection already satisfies the objective for that leg of the transmission.

## Edge cases

- **Whether a network segment with FIPS-encrypted endpoints still needs an additional encrypted tunnel layered on top.** One position holds that if every byte carrying CUI on that segment can be shown to be FIPS-encrypted, the segment-level protection is enough; the more conservative position assumes any device reachable on that segment can see the unencrypted traffic and requires transport encryption regardless. The outcome depends on the assessor.
- **Fax and standard telephone voice transmission of CUI.** Guidance on whether unencrypted fax and voice communication over standard telephone lines need separate protection is inconsistent between sources; treat this as an open item to confirm rather than assume either way.
- **Whether every inbound email needs to be verified as TLS-protected, or only email from known, connector-verified partners.** The verified-partner case is well established; unknown senders remain an unresolved gap.
- **Legacy equipment that cannot negotiate a current, encrypted file-sharing protocol.** Isolating that equipment on its own network segment is a documented mitigation, but whether isolation alone substitutes for encrypting its traffic depends on the assessor.
- **Rev 3 direction.** Source discussion notes the alternative-physical-safeguard option is expected to be removed in the next revision, meaning a plan built entirely around physical separation rather than encryption is a near-term reliance worth planning around, not the current Rev 2 rule.

## Evidence examples

- **Operating system configuration showing FIPS mode enabled together with enforced current-version TLS.** [a][c]
- **Mail flow connector configuration enforcing TLS for identified partner domains.** [c]
- **Common-carrier transmission service documentation describing dedicated separation, when relying on the physical-safeguard alternative.** [b][c]
- **Connectivity test results confirming a legacy unencrypted transmission path is closed or unavailable.** [c]

## Related requirements

- **3.13.11** (CUI Encryption) supplies the FIPS-validation standard that the cryptographic mechanisms identified under objective [a] here must meet.
- **3.13.15** (Communications Authenticity) often shares the same TLS configuration as this requirement, since a protected session needs both confidentiality and a verified, unchanging identity on each end.
- **3.1.12 and 3.1.13** (remote access) depend on this requirement's transmission protection whenever CUI moves over a remote connection.
