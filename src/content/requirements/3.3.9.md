## Requirement

"Limit management of audit logging functionality to a subset of privileged users."

## Objectives

[a] a subset of privileged users granted access to manage audit logging functionality is defined; and
[b] management of audit logging functionality is limited to the defined subset of privileged users.

## Sufficient

- **A written, named list of exactly which privileged accounts may configure or manage audit logging**, even when that list is short. The Assessment Guide discussion frames this as separating audit-management privilege from general privileged access, so the requirement is satisfied once such a list exists and access matches it. [a][b]
- **Very small organizations where the entire privileged staff, or nearly all of it, forms the defined subset.** Practitioner consensus accepts that a true separation between "audit managers" and "everyone else with privileged access" is not realistic once a company has only one or two system administrators; what matters is that the subset is written down, even if it equals essentially the whole IT staff, and that those individuals review each other's activity as a form of oversight. [a][b]
- **Two administrators in a small environment who both hold log-management rights and periodically review each other's actions along with everyone else's.** This mutual-review pattern is accepted as a workable substitute for a larger organization's separation of duties. [a][b]

## Insufficient

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Over-engineering

- **Constructing an elaborate formal argument for why "a subset" can mathematically include the entire set of privileged users, in place of simply defining and documenting a smaller group where the organization's size allows it.** The Assessment Guide's Discussion text is not meant to add requirements beyond the stated objectives, so a workable answer is a documented subset, however small the organization, rather than an argument for why no reduction is needed at all. Where true separation is genuinely impossible due to headcount, documenting that reality plainly is the proportionate response; building a formal justification around it is not required.

## Edge cases

- **Organizations with only one or two system administrators, where any meaningful separation between audit managers and other privileged users is not achievable.** The accepted position treats the defined subset as satisfied even when it equals the entire available IT staff, provided the subset is explicitly written down and coverage needs (such as backup access during absences) are accounted for; a second reviewer examining the same activity is treated as a reasonable stand-in for separation of duties in this scenario.

## Evidence examples

- **A written definition, in policy or the system security plan, naming the accounts that make up the privileged subset with audit-management rights.** [a]
- **Log platform or system configuration showing that only the defined subset actually holds management-level access.** [b]
- **Log review records showing mutual review among the small group of privileged users**, used as oversight in organizations too small for a larger separation of duties. [a][b]

## Related requirements

- **3.3.8** protects the audit information and tools this requirement's defined subset is trusted to manage; the two requirements together answer who may touch the logs and how those logs stay protected once touched.
- **3.1.5** supplies the least-privilege foundation this requirement narrows further, cutting the broader privileged-user population down to the smaller group with audit-management rights specifically.
- **3.1.6** separates privileged functions from non-privileged use generally, a pattern this requirement applies specifically to audit logging management.
