## Requirement

"Disable identifiers after a defined period of inactivity."

## Objectives

[a] a period of inactivity after which an identifier is disabled is defined; and
[b] identifiers are disabled after the defined period of inactivity.

## Sufficient

- **Scoping the inactivity-disabling practice to user accounts and identifiers.** That is the plain reading of the objective and where practitioner discussion converges. [a][b]
- **A documented inactivity period paired with a periodic check against last-login data**, disabling any account that has not been used within that period. This matches the Assessment Guide's own example of the practice. [a][b]

## Insufficient

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Over-engineering

- **Extending the same disabling logic to computer accounts, unused physical access cards, or other non-user identifiers.** Practitioner consensus treats this requirement as centered on user accounts; extending it further goes beyond the accepted scope.

## Edge cases

- **Extended leave, such as medical or parental leave, raises a practical question about an account that would otherwise trip the inactivity threshold.** Suspending the account rather than fully disabling or deleting it, while retaining any assigned device on inventory, is a workable approach, with reactivation once the individual returns.
- **Whether a mobile device that can hold locally downloaded material needs its own identifier-disabling logic depends on whether the device can retain data outside an approved, managed application.** If it can, the device likely needs to be treated as in scope rather than assumed exempt.

## Evidence examples

- **Policy defining the inactivity period after which an identifier is disabled.** [a]
- **Configuration or report showing accounts automatically disabled after the defined period of inactivity.** [b]

## Related requirements

- **3.5.5** governs reuse of the same identifiers this requirement disables; the two work together across an identifier's lifecycle.
- **3.1.1's** authorized-user roster is the reference point for deciding whether a disabled identifier should be reactivated or retired for good.
