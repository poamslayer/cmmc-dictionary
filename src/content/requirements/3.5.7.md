## Requirement

"Enforce a minimum password complexity and change of characters when new passwords are created."

## Objectives

[a] password complexity requirements are defined;
[b] password change of character requirements are defined;
[c] minimum password complexity requirements as defined are enforced when new passwords are created; and
[d] minimum password change of character requirements as defined are enforced when new passwords are created.

## Sufficient

- **A directory-enforced policy defining a minimum length and requiring at least three of the four character types (uppercase, lowercase, number, special character), with a rule that a changed password differ from the previous one by at least one character.** This combination, paired with multifactor authentication and no mandatory periodic password change, has passed a government audit. [a][b][c][d]
- **Multifactor authentication paired with a non-expiring password.** Once a second factor is required for every sign-in, some organizations no longer force periodic password resets and have passed assessment on that basis. [c][d]
- **A defined minimum password length raised above the older eight-character floor, with a documented justification that current practice has moved past that historical minimum.** This is an accepted way to both define and enforce complexity. [a][c]

## Insufficient

- **An eight-character minimum, while technically compliant with this requirement's objectives when defined and enforced, is viewed by some assessors as a weak security choice on its own merits.** The shorter length does not automatically fail the objective, but raising it is commonly recommended on security grounds separate from the compliance question.

## Over-engineering

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Edge cases

- **Whether a forced periodic password-reset schedule must accompany complexity rules is unsettled.** One organization reports an assessor insisting on at least some scheduled reset interval and pushing back on going without one; another reports passing with no forced password-change requirement at all once multifactor authentication was in place; the outcome depends on the assessor.
- **NIST SP 800-63B has moved toward recommending against forcing password resets purely on a fixed schedule**, on the reasoning that frequent forced changes can encourage predictable pattern changes and increase risk rather than reduce it. Practitioners are not unanimous about how much weight to give this yet, since assessment experience still sometimes expects a scheduled reset regardless of the newer guidance.

## Evidence examples

- **Directory group policy export** showing the defined minimum length and character-type requirements. [a][c]
- **Configuration showing enforcement of a minimum number of changed characters** between an old and new password. [b][d]
- **System Security Plan section documenting the defined password complexity and change-of-character rules.** [a][b]
- **Citation to current digital-identity guidance** supporting the organization's chosen approach to password expiration, where relevant. [c][d]

## Related requirements

- **3.5.3** layers multifactor authentication on top of the password factor this requirement governs, and is often the reason an organization no longer forces periodic password resets.
- **3.5.8** prohibits reusing recent passwords, a related but separate rule about password history rather than password shape.
- **3.5.9** governs the special case of a temporary password, which this requirement's complexity rules still apply to once it is replaced with a permanent one.
