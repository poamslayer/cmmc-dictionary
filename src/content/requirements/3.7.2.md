## Requirement

"Provide controls on the tools, techniques, mechanisms, and personnel used to conduct system maintenance."

## Objectives

[a] tools used to conduct system maintenance are controlled;
[b] techniques used to conduct system maintenance are controlled;
[c] mechanisms used to conduct system maintenance are controlled; and
[d] personnel used to conduct system maintenance are controlled.

## Sufficient

- **This requirement's scope is maintenance tooling that sits outside the system's own authorization boundary.** Where an external service provider's management platform is itself treated as part of the boundary, that platform's own tooling is addressed through the boundary's normal controls rather than as a separate maintenance tool. [a][c]
- **Built-in diagnostic utilities that ship as part of the operating system, rather than separate tools brought in from outside, are addressed through other access and configuration requirements.** They do not need a maintenance-specific control layered on top. [a]
- **Where an external provider installs remote monitoring and management tooling to perform maintenance, that access is controlled the same way any other externally originating application access is controlled, through terms defining what the tool may reach and do on the system.** [a][d]
- **Documentation and process descriptions of how maintenance is carried out address the technique objective, while automated scripts and scheduled jobs that actually perform maintenance actions address the mechanism objective.** Keeping the two separate in the policy makes both objectives easier for an assessor to verify. [b][c]
- **A maintenance policy naming the specific tools, mechanisms, and personnel authorized to perform maintenance, cross-referenced against the asset inventory.** This gives an assessor a concrete way to check who and what is authorized rather than relying on an interview alone. [a][c][d]

## Insufficient

- **A maintenance program with no record of which tools were approved, or with tool access left open to anyone holding general system access rather than a defined set of personnel.** This does not demonstrate that tools or personnel are actually controlled, even where maintenance itself is happening correctly. [a][d]
- **Relying on an informal, undocumented understanding of who performs maintenance.** The personnel-control objective needs a documented list an assessor can check, not a description offered only in an interview. [d]

## Over-engineering

- **Building a separate formal control process for every native operating system utility used incidentally during maintenance work.** Basic connectivity or listing commands built into the operating system are addressed by the access and configuration requirements that already govern the system, not by a maintenance-specific control on top of them. [a]

## Edge cases

- **Whether an external provider's persistent management connection counts as an in-scope maintenance tool, or whether the provider itself is part of the authorization boundary, is a scoping judgment rather than a settled rule.** Treating the provider as part of the boundary shifts its tools out of this requirement's reach and into the boundary's own access controls instead; the outcome depends on how the boundary is drawn and documented.

## Evidence examples

- **System maintenance policy naming approved tools, techniques, mechanisms, and personnel.** [a][b][c][d]
- **Asset or tool inventory cross-referenced against the maintenance policy's authorized list.** [a][c]
- **External provider terms of access covering what a remote monitoring and management tool may reach and do.** [a][d]
- **Process documentation describing how routine maintenance techniques are carried out.** [b]
- **Records of maintenance tool inspection or currency, showing tools are kept updated and free of malicious code.** [a]

## Related requirements

- **3.7.1** is the activity this requirement puts controls around; maintenance has to be occurring before its tools and personnel can be controlled.
- **3.1.20** governs external connections generally and supplies the terms-of-access approach used when an outside provider's maintenance tooling reaches into the environment.
- **3.7.6** supervises maintenance personnel who lack standing access authorization, which overlaps with the personnel-control objective here for anyone without prior authorization.
