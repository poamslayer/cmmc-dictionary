## Requirement

"Limit physical access to organizational information systems, equipment, and the respective operating environments to authorized individuals."

## Objectives

[a] authorized individuals allowed physical access are identified;
[b] physical access to organizational systems is limited to authorized individuals;
[c] physical access to equipment is limited to authorized individuals; and
[d] physical access to operating environments is limited to authorized individuals.

## Sufficient

- **A light physical barrier, such as a fenced enclosure with a locked gate, around shop-floor equipment**, when the CUI itself (printed drawings or documents) is stored in a locked cabinet by the end of the day. What needs limiting is access to the CUI, and a fence plus a locked cabinet is accepted as doing that. [c][d]
- **A fenced or caged enclosure functioning as a second layer inside an already badge-controlled, camera-covered area.** Practitioner consensus treats stacking a lighter barrier on top of an existing controlled perimeter as adequate rather than requiring solid construction at every layer. [c][d]
- **A written work-from-home or telework policy, backed by user training, specifying how CUI is handled at the alternate site**, since the physical environment of a home cannot be built out the way an office can. Largely a policy-driven control for this scenario. [a][b][c][d]
- **Open bay or garage doors left open for heat or ventilation reasons, paired with signage and staff training to challenge people who don't belong**, plus a documented, periodically refreshed training plan and a written rationale for the arrangement. [b][d]
- **A written policy prohibiting the printing of CUI, reinforced with signage at the printer itself**, accepted in place of a technical print-blocking control. [c]
- **Non-CUI personnel sharing a CUI-adjacent space, documented and treated as in-scope**, carrying the same screening and other applicable controls as CUI-authorized personnel, or alternatively managed as escorted visitors rather than physically separated from the space. [a][d]

## Insufficient

- **Treating manufactured parts or other physical goods as the thing this requirement protects.** Physical items are not information and cannot be CUI by themselves; the access that needs limiting is access to printed documents, drawings, or displayed information that actually carries CUI, not access to hardware or finished goods. [c]

## Over-engineering

- **Building a separate physical enclosure for a small handful of staff who have no CUI duties, just to keep them out of a shared space.** Documenting them as in-scope with matching controls, or applying alternate-work-site-style measures such as obscured screens and discretion in conversation, reaches the same objective without a build-out. [a][d]
- **Requiring floor-to-ceiling solid walls for a manufacturing enclosure** when the CUI documents inside are locked in a cabinet at day's end; a fenced enclosure with a locked gate meets the same objective for far less cost. [c][d]
- **Deploying data-loss-prevention or print-blocking technology to stop CUI from being printed**, when a written no-print policy paired with printer signage already satisfies the objective. [c]

## Edge cases

- **Whether a lighter barrier, such as a chain-link-style fence, is adequate around equipment inside an already-controlled area, or whether solid construction is required.** One position treats a lighter barrier as insufficient on its own and calls for solid walls; the more common practitioner position treats a lighter enclosure, layered inside a badge-controlled perimeter with camera coverage, as sufficient, particularly once CUI documents are locked away each day. The outcome depends on the assessor.
- **How "organizational systems" (objective [b]) is meant to differ from "equipment" (objective [c]).** The line between the two categories is not sharply drawn in the source material; in practice both are read broadly enough to cover the system's components and the physical spaces where CUI could be seen or overheard, so an implementation that limits access at the space level tends to satisfy both.

## Evidence examples

- **A written work-from-home or alternate-work-site policy** with an associated training record. [a][b][c][d]
- **Signage at printers stating CUI reproduction is prohibited**, paired with the underlying policy it reinforces. [c]
- **Badge access logs for facility entry.** [a][b]
- **Camera footage covering equipment enclosures and controlled areas.** [c][d]
- **A documented training record on challenging unrecognized individuals**, alongside a visitor sign-in process. [a][d]
- **Locked cabinets used for end-of-day CUI storage**, paired with the fenced or caged enclosure around the equipment area. [c][d]
