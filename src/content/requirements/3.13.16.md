## Requirement

"Protect the confidentiality of CUI at rest."

## Objectives

[a] the confidentiality of CUI at rest is protected.

## Sufficient

- **Full disk encryption using a FIPS-validated algorithm, deployed to every computer that stores CUI.** Assessment Guide discussion presents this as one of the standard paths to satisfying the objective, alongside physical protection. [a]
- **Physical protection alone, for equipment that never leaves a physically controlled area.** Practitioner consensus treats encryption as one acceptable mechanism, not the only one; equipment that stays inside company-controlled space can rely on physical access control instead of encryption. [a]
- **A documented sign-out procedure for a device that cannot support encryption**, requiring the device to remain with the person who checked it out and to be returned to locked storage when not in use, verified by a periodic audit of the sign-out record. Assessment Guide discussion accepts this alongside encryption as a way to protect confidentiality at rest. [a]
- **Administrative controls that keep CUI from ever landing on a device**, for an asset category defined by processing or transmitting CUI without storing it. Practitioner consensus treats this as satisfying the objective because there is no CUI at rest to protect on that asset in the first place. [a]

## Insufficient

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Over-engineering

- **Encrypting an asset in a category defined by never storing CUI at rest.** Practitioner consensus holds that if CUI is found at rest on such an asset, the asset no longer fits that category; encrypting it does not address a gap, because the category's own definition already rules out CUI being there. [a]
- **Requiring FIPS-validated encryption on a device that never stores CUI.** The FIPS validation bar attaches to algorithms actually protecting CUI at rest, not to every device in the environment regardless of what it holds. [a]

## Edge cases

- **Work-from-home equipment loses the physical-protection option.** Practitioner consensus treats any device that leaves an area under the organization's physical access control as needing cryptographic protection, since the reduced physical control at a home office does not meet the same bar. [a]
- **Mobile devices and mobile computing platforms sit outside this requirement's physical-protection alternative.** A separate requirement (3.1.19) mandates encryption specifically for that device class, so the "physical protection instead of encryption" path described above is understood to apply only to non-mobile equipment.
- **Externally hosted or cloud-based storage does not map cleanly onto either the encryption or the physical-protection path**, since the underlying standard was written assuming on-premises infrastructure with remote access. How to treat FIPS-encrypted backups held by an external service provider has not settled into one accepted answer; the outcome depends on the assessor.

## Evidence examples

- **Full disk encryption deployment records** showing a FIPS-validated algorithm configured across company computers that store CUI. [a]
- **A completed device sign-out log** paired with the written policy it enforces, showing a non-encryptable device stayed with its authorized user and was returned to secured storage. [a]
- **A data handling policy or inventory record** showing that a given asset category is administratively kept from storing CUI at rest. [a]
- **Physical access control records**, such as badge logs or key control tracking, for a facility area holding stationary equipment relying on physical protection. [a]

## Related requirements

- **3.1.19** requires encryption specifically for mobile devices and mobile computing platforms, narrowing this requirement's physical-protection option for that device class.
- **3.13.11** sets the FIPS validation bar that any cryptographic mechanism used to satisfy this requirement must meet.
- **3.8.6** governs encryption for portable and removable media, overlapping with this requirement once such media leaves a physically controlled area.
- **3.8.9** requires confidentiality protection for CUI held at backup storage locations, extending this requirement's logic to backup copies.
