## Requirement

"Monitor organizational systems, including inbound and outbound communications traffic, to detect attacks and indicators of potential attacks."

## Objectives

[a] the system is monitored to detect attacks and indicators of potential attacks;
[b] inbound communications traffic is monitored to detect attacks and indicators of potential attacks; and
[c] outbound communications traffic is monitored to detect attacks and indicators of potential attacks.

## Sufficient

- **Firewall-generated alerts for malware, intrusion, or attack indicators, configured to notify designated personnel.** Accepted when the alerting covers both inbound and outbound traffic and someone is actually reviewing the notifications, not just receiving them. [a][b][c]
- **A log platform generating daily alerts against defined criteria**, such as failed remote access attempts, paired with a documented review step. [a]
- **Gateway security services (antivirus, anti-spyware, intrusion prevention) enabled across all network zones**, feeding alerts that are actively watched. [a][b][c]
- **An intrusion detection or prevention system**, which provides an active monitoring capability that a firewall's own rule enforcement does not. [a]

## Insufficient

- **A firewall's own traffic blocking and allowing under its rule set, treated as monitoring on its own.** Enforcing rules is not the same as observing and reporting on activity; only a layer that generates alerts someone reviews satisfies the objective. [a][b][c]
- **Alert-generating tools that nobody reviews.** The missing piece is a documented review step, not the presence of alerts; generating notifications without a person or process acting on them fails the objective. [a]
- **A content filter that only blocks traffic, with no alerting or review capability behind it.** [a]

## Over-engineering

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Edge cases

- **A very small organization weighing a contracted monitoring service against buying alert-generating tools in-house**, including whether a detection platform licensed for a single endpoint is adequate, remains an open question in practice; the outcome depends on the assessor.
- **An environment built from individual laptops and cloud workspaces rather than a managed network perimeter** raises unresolved questions about how outbound traffic gets monitored and whether endpoint protection alone covers the objective; the outcome depends on the assessor.
- **Where nearly all CUI-related work happens on government-furnished systems and CUI is never stored locally by policy**, with transfer limited to encrypted channels, the monitoring boundary for the company's own systems narrows, though how far it narrows is not settled.

## Evidence examples

- **Firewall alert rule configuration for malware, attack, and intrusion indicators.** [a][b][c]
- **A daily security log alert report**, for example summarizing successful and failed remote access attempts. [a]
- **Gateway security service configuration enabled across all network zones.** [a][b][c]
- **A record showing alerts reach and are reviewed by designated personnel**, such as an email trail or a ticketing history. [a]
- **Intrusion detection or prevention system configuration and alert history.** [a]

## Related requirements

- **3.3.1** requires the audit records this requirement's monitoring activity draws from and feeds into.
- **3.13.1** and boundary protection define where inbound and outbound traffic can be observed in the first place.
- **3.6.1** picks up incident response actions once this requirement's monitoring surfaces a confirmed attack.
