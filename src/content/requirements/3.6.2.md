## Requirement

"Track, document, and report incidents to designated officials and/or authorities both internal and external to the organization."

## Objectives

[a] incidents are tracked;
[b] incidents are documented;
[c] authorities to whom incidents are to be reported are identified;
[d] organizational officials to whom incidents are to be reported are identified;
[e] identified authorities are notified of incidents; and
[f] identified organizational officials are notified of incidents.

## Sufficient

- **A written statement that no incident has met the organization's own reporting threshold, naming the standard applied and confirming that no event since a given date has triggered it.** Practitioner consensus accepts this as satisfying the external-notification objective in the absence of any qualifying incident, since a notification obligation that never triggers has nothing further to demonstrate. [e]
- **Incident category thresholds built by using an existing government incident-handling directive only as a structural reference, then defining an organization-specific reportable-incident category tied to the actual DFARS reporting trigger and other applicable rules.** This has been accepted as a way to satisfy the authority and criteria objectives without adopting a framework built for a different kind of organization. [c][d]
- **A tiering scheme that routes low-severity events to internal IT handling only, mid-severity events to elevated internal reporting, and high-severity events meeting the reporting trigger to external reporting.** This gives an assessor a clear, documented mapping from event severity to the required notification. [c][d][e][f]
- **An incident tracking log or ticketing record maintained even when it shows zero incidents meeting the external threshold.** The record itself satisfies the tracking and documentation objectives independent of whether any incident required escalation. [a][b]

## Insufficient

- **An incident record with no named list of internal officials or external authorities to notify.** Objectives [c] and [d] stay unmet even when incidents are otherwise tracked, because the destination of a report has to be defined in advance rather than decided case by case when an incident occurs.
- **Tracking incidents only through informal, unsaved conversations rather than a persistent record.** The documentation objective calls for a record that can be retrieved later, not a recollection of what happened. [b]

## Over-engineering

- **Forwarding every automatically blocked or quarantined malicious-code detection to external authorities, on the assumption that any malware event is reportable.** Malicious code that never affected the system and never reached CUI data falls outside the external reporting trigger; documenting the determination that an event was contained is enough, without the added effort of escalating it. [e]
- **Basing an internal reporting-category framework entirely on a directive built for a different kind of organization, reproduced in full rather than adapted.** A shorter, organization-specific category scheme tied to the actual DFARS reporting trigger reaches the same objectives at a fraction of the documentation cost. [c][d]

## Edge cases

- **Whether a blocked or quarantined malicious-code detection is reportable turns on whether it affected the system, or whether the system withstood the attempt, and whether it reached CUI data on the affected host.** An event contained before either of those things happened can be documented as ruled out rather than escalated, though the record of that determination should still exist.
- **Malware samples can be submitted through the government's incident reporting channel independently of filing a full incident report.** This voluntary submission path is separate from, and does not by itself satisfy, the reporting trigger this requirement addresses.

## Evidence examples

- **Documented incident category framework mapping severity tiers to internal-only, elevated-internal, and external reporting.** [c][d]
- **Incident tracking log or ticketing system, including entries showing no incident met the external reporting threshold.** [a][b]
- **Written no-incident statement citing the applicable reporting standard and confirming no incident has met it.** [e]
- **Records of submissions through the government's incident reporting channel, and reports filed within the 72-hour DFARS window.** [e]
- **List of designated internal officials and external authorities maintained as part of the incident response plan.** [c][d]

## Related requirements

- **3.6.1** establishes the capability that produces the incidents this requirement tracks and reports; without it there is nothing to document.
- **3.6.3** verifies through testing that the tracking and reporting workflow actually functions when exercised, not only on paper.
- **3.3.1** and related audit logging requirements often supply the detail recorded in the incident documentation this requirement requires.
