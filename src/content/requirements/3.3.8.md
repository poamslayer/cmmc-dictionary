## Requirement

"Protect audit information and audit logging tools from unauthorized access, modification, and deletion."

## Objectives

[a] audit information is protected from unauthorized access;
[b] audit information is protected from unauthorized modification;
[c] audit information is protected from unauthorized deletion;
[d] audit logging tools are protected from unauthorized access;
[e] audit logging tools are protected from unauthorized modification; and
[f] audit logging tools are protected from unauthorized deletion.

## Sufficient

- **Access control as the mechanism satisfying all six objectives.** Practitioner consensus points to a single access-restriction scheme covering both the audit records and the tools used to view or manage them, with different privilege levels for viewing versus deleting, and no standing allowance for anyone to alter records once written. [a][b][c][d][e][f]
- **A defined administrative role for audit and log management, paired with the specific accounts assigned to it.** Showing both the role's stated permissions and who currently holds that role is accepted as evidence that access is both defined and enforced. [d][e][f]
- **A single administrator with log-platform access in a very small organization, combined with a second person who independently receives periodic log summaries.** Practitioner consensus accepts this structure in micro-sized companies where a larger separation of duties is impractical, since a second set of eyes still exists even without a dedicated audit role. [a][d]
- **Central log forwarding that removes local, easily altered copies from general reach**, restricting who can view the aggregated record; the Assessment Guide discussion frames this centralization plus restricted viewing as a core protection pattern. [a][b][c]
- **Encrypted, access-restricted backups of the audit repository**, addressing the deletion and access objectives even if the primary system is compromised. Assessment Guide example. [a][b][c]

## Insufficient

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Over-engineering

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Edge cases

- **Whether objectives [d], [e], and [f] need distinct, separately demonstrated controls from [a], [b], and [c].** Practitioner consensus treats a single access-control scheme as sufficient to cover the logging tools the same way it covers the records themselves: if only authorized users can reach the tools at all, that same restriction is read as also covering unauthorized modification and deletion of those tools, without a separate control for each letter.
- **Very small organizations where only one person holds administrative access to the log platform.** The accepted pattern is one administrator with platform access, plus a second person who reviews periodic exported reports rather than holding platform access directly; no definitive assessor ruling is documented for this exact structure, so the outcome depends on the assessor.

## Evidence examples

- **Role definition documenting the audit or security-administrator role's stated permissions.** [d][e][f]
- **Account or group assignment showing exactly who holds that role.** [d][e][f]
- **Log platform or SIEM access configuration screenshot restricting viewing and management rights.** [a][d]
- **Distribution record of periodic log summaries sent to a secondary reviewer** in smaller organizations without a dedicated audit role. [a]
- **Backup configuration showing encryption and restricted access for archived audit data.** [a][b][c]

## Related requirements

- **3.3.7** supplies the reliable, synchronized timestamps that make the protected audit records in this requirement meaningful to review.
- **3.3.9** narrows who may manage audit logging functionality, working alongside this requirement's broader protection of the records and tools themselves.
- **3.1.5** and **3.1.6** provide the least-privilege and privileged-account controls this requirement's access restrictions typically draw on.
- **3.8.9** protects backup copies of information generally, which extends to encrypted, access-restricted backups of audit data used as evidence here.
