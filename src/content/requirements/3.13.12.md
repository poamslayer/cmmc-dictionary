## Requirement

"Prohibit remote activation of collaborative computing devices and provide indication of devices in use to users present at the device."

## Objectives

[a] collaborative computing devices are identified;
[b] collaborative computing devices provide indication to users of devices in use; and
[c] remote activation of collaborative computing devices is prohibited.

## Sufficient

- **Reading laptop and workstation built-in cameras and microphones as in scope, not just conference-room equipment.** The Assessment Guide's discussion names networked white boards, cameras, and microphones without limiting them to room systems, and excludes only dedicated video conferencing units that require a participant to answer a call before activating. An organization with no conference-room hardware still has to identify its endpoint cameras and microphones under objective [a]. [a]
- **Physical camera covers, laptop camera sliders, or even a simple opaque cover over the lens**, accepted as adequate to prohibit remote activation without additional software. [c]
- **Conference-room or collaboration units that display a visible indicator, a light or an on-screen notification, whenever their camera or microphone is active**, satisfying the indication-of-use objective. [b]
- **Requiring a participant to accept an incoming call before a device's camera or microphone activate**, mirroring how a phone call works rather than allowing a device to activate silently. [c]
- **Disabling the auto-accept setting on room collaboration systems**, so an incoming connection request cannot activate the device without someone present accepting it. [c]
- **Built-in device indicator lights counted toward the indication objective, alongside the meeting software's own on-screen indicator.** [b]

## Insufficient

- **A conference-room unit configured to auto-accept incoming connection requests**, which allows its camera and microphone to activate without anyone at the device consenting. [c]
- **A device with no indicator light and no on-screen or physical notification when its camera or microphone are active**, leaving anyone present with no way to know it is in use. [b]
- **Treating cameras and microphones as out of scope simply because the organization has no dedicated conference-room hardware.** The exclusion in the Assessment Guide's discussion applies only to dedicated video conferencing units, not to endpoint hardware generally, so this reading does not hold up. [a]

## Over-engineering

- **Building a dedicated network segment isolating every device with a camera or microphone**, when configuring the device itself to require call acceptance already satisfies the remote-activation objective. [c]
- **A device policy that disables cameras only during remote-desktop sessions**, when the exposure worth addressing is a room system that auto-accepts incoming calls, not an end-user device being remotely controlled during an already-authenticated support session. [c]

## Edge cases

- **Whether laptop and workstation built-in cameras and microphones count as collaborative computing devices at all.** One reading treats the requirement as concerned mainly with dedicated conference-room equipment; the Assessment Guide's exclusion clause, which carves out only dedicated video conferencing units requiring call acceptance, does not survive that reading, since excluding a specific category has no meaning if that category were the only thing ever in scope. The stronger reading brings endpoint cameras and microphones into scope for every organization.
- **Remote-support tooling that lets an administrator open a camera application on a user's machine during an already-established remote session.** One position treats this as ordinary remote access rather than a collaborative computing device, since the connection itself, not the camera, is the thing being controlled; a more cautious position still expects the person at the device to consent to or be warned about the remote session, which feeds into separate access-control requirements rather than this one. The outcome depends on the assessor.
- **Whether always-listening smart-speaker style devices fall inside this requirement's definition of a collaborative computing device.** Not clearly settled in available discussion; treat it as an open item to confirm rather than assume either way.

## Evidence examples

- **Inventory identifying devices with cameras or microphones capable of collaborative use, including endpoint hardware.** [a]
- **Configuration screenshot showing a conference or collaboration device requires call acceptance before activating.** [c]
- **Photograph or asset record showing a physical camera cover or slider in place.** [c]
- **Documentation of the on-screen or hardware indicator each device type provides when active.** [b]

## Related requirements

- **3.1.12** (remote access monitoring and control) and this requirement both address the risk of an unauthorized party reaching into the organization's systems from outside, one at the connection level and one at the device level.
- **3.13.13** (mobile code) and **3.13.14** (VoIP) sit in the same part of the family as this requirement, each governing a specific communication or collaboration technology with its own control-and-monitor or activation logic.
