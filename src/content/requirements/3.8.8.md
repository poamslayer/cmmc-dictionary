## Requirement

"Prohibit the use of portable storage devices when such devices have no identifiable owner."

## Objectives

[a] the use of portable storage devices is prohibited when such devices have no identifiable owner.

## Sufficient

- **Treating the organization itself as the owner of issued devices, rather than requiring a named individual owner in every case.** Practitioner consensus reads "identifiable owner" broadly enough that organization-level ownership satisfies the objective when devices are issued to employees under that umbrella. [a]
- **Flexible ownership assignment to a team, facility, or work area, not only to one named individual.** Assigning a drive to a specific individual and keeping an inventory of who received it is the most common approach, but it is not the only acceptable one. [a]
- **A data-loss-prevention tool configured to allow read-only access to any portable storage device by default, with write access granted only by exception to organization-issued drives**, paired with policy prohibiting the use of non-issued drives. [a]
- **Documentation recording which device was issued to which owner**, kept as part of the device inventory. [a]

## Insufficient

- **Permitting any portable storage device to connect without establishing ownership or applying a technical control.** This does not meet the requirement's prohibition on devices without an identifiable owner. [a]
- **A policy-only approach with no technical enforcement behind it.** Practitioner consensus expects both an administrative control, such as policy and training, and a technical control, such as a device-control or data-loss-prevention rule, working together rather than policy alone. [a]

## Over-engineering

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Edge cases

- **Granting read-only use of every portable storage device regardless of origin, while reserving write permission for organization-issued drives alone.** This design, paired with administrative policy and training against plugging in outside drives, has been floated as workable but remains something individual assessors are still asked to weigh in on rather than a settled answer; the outcome depends on the assessor.
- **Ownership resting with a team, a facility, or a work area instead of one named person.** That level of ownership still meets the identifiable-owner objective; nothing requires tracing the device to a single individual by name.

## Evidence examples

- **Inventory list** naming each portable storage device and the owner it was assigned to, whether that owner is a person, a team, or a broader unit. [a]
- **Data-loss-prevention configuration** documenting the default read-only setting and the exception process granting write access to issued devices. [a]
- **Policy document** prohibiting the use of non-issued portable storage devices. [a]
- **Device issuance record** showing which device was issued to which owner. [a]
- **Training record** covering portable storage device policy for employees. [a]

## Related requirements

- **3.8.7** controls the use of removable media generally; this requirement adds a narrower ownership condition on top of that control.
- **3.8.4** supports the same identifiable-owner objective when a device's physical marking also names its owner.
- **3.1.1** and **3.1.2** supply the underlying authorized-user determination for who may be issued a device in the first place.
