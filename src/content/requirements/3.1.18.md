## Requirement

"Control connection of mobile devices."

## Objectives

- [a] mobile devices that process, store, or transmit CUI are identified;
- [b] mobile device connections are authorized; and
- [c] mobile device connections are monitored and logged.

## Sufficient

- **Manage phones and tablets through application management rather than full device management**, containerizing organizational data inside approved applications without requiring the organization to own the hardware; this has carried mixed personal-device fleets through assessment when paired with documented policy. [b][c]
- **Prohibit mobile devices from reaching the CUI environment at all**, and describe that as a met finding naming the specific technical controls enforcing the prohibition, rather than marking the requirement not applicable. [a][b]
- **Route CUI-related email through an address deliberately not configured on any phone**, removing those phones from this requirement's reach for that data flow, provided the restriction is actually enforced rather than merely requested. [a]
- **Configure phones as unmanaged but capable of a targeted remote wipe of the application holding CUI**, with encryption enforced for CUI reached through a government cloud tenant, and document all of it in policy. [b][c]

## Insufficient

- **Marking this requirement, or the related mobile encryption requirement, not applicable because mobile devices are prohibited**: a prohibition still has to be described as a met control, not an inapplicable one, since the government, not the contractor, grants permission to mark something not applicable. [a][b]
- **Classifying a phone that actually stores or processes CUI as a risk-managed asset** merely because an application-management tool protects it; if the device holds CUI, it is a CUI asset regardless of the containerization technology. [a]
- **Assuming an application-management container's encryption alone satisfies a FIPS-validation requirement** without confirming the container creates a validated encrypted store; one assessment held firmly to requiring a validated cryptographic module on the device itself. [c]

## Over-engineering

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Edge cases

- Whether a laptop counts as a "mobile device" under this requirement is disputed outside close practitioner circles even though the technical definition, a small form factor with self-contained power designed for wireless operation, separates a laptop, a mobile computing platform, from a phone or tablet; at least one live assessment initially pushed back on this distinction before accepting it, so the definition is worth having ready.
- A laptop not used for CUI work can be treated as a risk-managed asset with an explanation of how it is kept away from CUI data, but a laptop that is used for CUI carries requirements, such as multifactor authentication, that do not apply the same way to a phone or tablet.
- Not every mobile device model has an available FIPS-validated cryptographic module, which complicates listing certain devices as CUI assets when a validated module cannot be found for that hardware; this is an open practical problem rather than a resolved one.
- Application management for phones and tablets has carried assessments in a government-community cloud tenant; the same approach in a fully commercial tenant does not carry the same track record.

## Evidence examples

- Application-management or device-management policy documentation showing which applications and devices may hold CUI. [a][b]
- Configuration export showing app protection policies enforcing encryption and blocking data movement outside the managed application. [b][c]
- Written policy prohibiting mobile device access to the CUI environment, paired with the technical control enforcing it, for example a conditional access rule blocking mobile platforms. [a][b]
- Mobile device inventory or exception list identifying which, if any, devices are authorized to hold CUI. [a]

## Related requirements

- 3.1.16 and 3.1.17 govern the wireless connection most mobile devices use to reach the network this requirement controls.
- 3.1.19 requires encryption of any CUI that ends up on an identified mobile device or computing platform.
- 3.5.3 requires multifactor authentication for network access, which applies differently to a managed laptop than to a phone or tablet under this requirement.
