## Requirement

"Enforce safeguarding measures for CUI at alternate work sites."

## Objectives

[a] safeguarding measures for CUI are defined for alternate work sites; and
[b] safeguarding measures for CUI are enforced for alternate work sites.

## Sufficient

- **A working definition of "controlled environment" borrowed from the public CUI program regulation**: any space where the person holding the CUI judges that physical or procedural safeguards, such as a barrier or a managed entry point, are enough to keep the material from unauthorized access or disclosure. Alternate work sites are measured against that same bar. [a][b]
- **Policy and training filling the gap left by "defined" being an organization-set parameter.** As long as the resulting posture meets the requirement's intent of protecting CUI outside the office, the organization gets to set the specifics. [a]
- **A written remote-work policy tying the alternate-work-site expectations together**, even where no single piece of control text demands a policy by that exact name; it is how organizations satisfy both objectives at once in practice. [a][b]
- **An encrypted VPN tunnel back to the corporate network, paired with mandatory user training**, as a realistic enforcement approach for a home or travel worksite. [b]
- **A home office or hotel room, using a locked door as the physical boundary**, with CUI kept in the user's possession or in a locked container. A wall and a locked door count as a legitimate physical boundary. [a][b]
- **Full-disk encryption, patch management, anti-virus protection, and VPN access requiring multi-factor authentication for remote devices.** Assessment Guide example. [b]
- **Most other NIST SP 800-171 protections traveling with the corporate-managed device and its connection**, letting an organization inherit much of its existing control implementation for a person working from home rather than building a parallel set of controls. [b]

## Insufficient

- **Printing CUI on an unprotected or shared printer**, such as a hotel business center or a client site's reception printer. Public telework guidance limiting unnecessary printing does not authorize printing on any available printer; it still has to satisfy this requirement along with the facility and physical-media controls printing otherwise depends on. [b]
- **Relying only on physical possession of a laptop and turning its screen away from view in a public setting**, without an actual physical boundary such as a wall or a locked door. Weaker than what most organizations are prepared to defend as meeting the objective. [b]

## Over-engineering

- **Defending against a highly deliberate physical surveillance threat**, such as someone specifically observing a screen from a distance in public. That concern belongs to classified-information handling; CUI calls for moderate, reasonable protections, not that level of lockdown. [b]
- **Reading "safeguarding measures" to mean every one of the 110 requirements applies unmodified at an alternate work site.** If that were the intent, this would not exist as its own narrower requirement. [a][b]
- **Ending remote work entirely as a compliance strategy.** That goes well past what a moderate-confidentiality information type calls for. [b]
- **Building a real-time geolocation system to block use from a coffee shop or an airplane.** A written policy prohibiting those locations accomplishes the same enforcement objective without the technical complexity. [b]
- **Treating foreign-travel restrictions as something this requirement reaches.** Nothing in the assessment objectives touches travel controls; that concern belongs to export-compliance obligations, not this requirement. [a][b]

## Edge cases

- **How far a home office's scope extends is genuinely open**, including whether the home internet connection, the home router, or a dedicated line needs its own controls. Most organizations resolve this by drawing the boundary at the company-managed device and its direct connection back to the enterprise, rather than trying to secure the whole household network.
- **A virtual desktop approach can scope an endpoint device down to a lighter asset category, but it does not resolve what physical environment the user is connecting from while traveling or in public.** The two questions, endpoint scoping and alternate-work-site safeguarding, stay separate.
- **For an organization whose only CUI assets sit inside a cloud service provider's data center, the sibling requirements 3.10.1 through 3.10.5 are the ones an organization can point back to the provider's own control set for; this one stays with the organization.** The provider secures its own data center, not an employee's kitchen table. Because reaching a cloud-hosted system counts as remote access unless someone is physically inside that data center, a fully cloud-native, remote organization ends up treating nearly every access as coming from an alternate work site, which raises the stakes on this requirement precisely for organizations that assumed the cloud absolved them of physical security. [a][b]

## Evidence examples

- **A written remote-work or alternate-work-site policy** defining approved site types and required safeguards. [a]
- **A user training record or signed acknowledgment** covering CUI handling while teleworking. [a][b]
- **VPN configuration evidence** showing an encrypted tunnel and multi-factor authentication for remote connections. [b]
- **Device encryption configuration** for equipment used at alternate work sites. [b]
- **A policy statement on printing restrictions at alternate work sites**, consistent with the organization's broader CUI-printing controls. [b]

## Related requirements

- **3.10.1 through 3.10.5** cover the primary facility; this is the one requirement in the family that a cloud-native organization cannot inherit from its provider, since it falls on wherever the organization's people actually work.
- **3.1.12** governs the remote access session itself; this requirement governs the physical environment that session originates from.
- **3.10.1** shares the printing scenario that shows up as a common misreading of public telework guidance under this requirement.
