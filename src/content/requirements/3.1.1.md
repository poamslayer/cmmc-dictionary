## Requirement

"Limit information system access to authorized users, processes acting on behalf of authorized users, or devices (including other information systems)."

## Objectives

[a] authorized users are identified;
[b] processes acting on behalf of authorized users are identified;
[c] devices (and other systems) authorized to connect to the system are identified;
[d] system access is limited to authorized users;
[e] system access is limited to processes acting on behalf of authorized users; and
[f] system access is limited to authorized devices (including other systems).

## Sufficient

- **A maintained roster of authorized users, service accounts, and devices, kept outside the identity provider itself.** Assessors have accepted this as the authoritative record for objectives [a] through [c], typically carrying a name or account identifier, the business justification, and a validation date. [a][b][c]
- **Non-person (service) account inventories listing the permissions each account holds**, paired with a note on what the account does and how it was approved, when the documentation ties the account to a business purpose. [b]
- **Firewall-level device restriction, by MAC address or IP allowlist**, accepted as technical enforcement for device authorization. [f]
- **Periodic (for example biweekly) network scans compared against the approved device list**, with a named reviewer who removes anything unrecognized; accepted for smaller environments, with the caveat that it does not scale to a much larger user base. [c][f]
- **An SSP written at the level of an asset category, such as the whole laptop fleet or the full set of security protection assets, instead of one entry per physical device.** This is the accepted level of granularity; listing controls machine by machine adds redundancy without adding assurance. [a][c]
- **Policy plus periodic review of device connection logs**, used in place of a device management platform, accepted for very small, cloud-only organizations with fewer than ten users. [c][f]
- **A managed application container on an otherwise unmanaged device.** Mobile management containerization of a mail or line-of-business app lets the container carry the in-scope controls while the underlying device sits in a lower-scoping category. [f]

## Insufficient

- **Pointing to the identity directory's user list as the authorization record.** A directory shows who currently has an account, not who was approved to have one; an account an intruder creates would look identical to a legitimate one under that logic. Assessors expect a separate source-of-truth record. [a]
- **Verbal approval with no record kept.** Assessors cannot validate an approval that happened in conversation with nothing written down at the time it occurred. [a][d]
- **Policy alone, with no technical enforcement, to limit which devices connect.** Without a technical control and a way to verify it is working, this arrangement breaks down quickly once the user count grows past a handful of people. [f]
- **Physical security as the primary control for device authorization.** Physical access restrictions are rarely accepted as the primary mechanism; some form of authentication is expected as the first line of control. [f]
- **Asserting the requirement is met because staff informally know who should have access.** This reasoning does not survive an outside assessor who was not present when access decisions were made. [a][d]

## Over-engineering

- **Disabling Bluetooth network-wide.** Only the mobile-code requirement even mentions it, and only as something to consider, not mandate; blocking file-transfer profiles through endpoint management addresses the underlying concern at far lower cost.
- **Building automated detection and blocking for every new service account.** The objective only asks for a roster: which service accounts exist, what they do, how they were authorized. A maintained list answers that; automated enforcement is not required.
- **Bringing customer or external-party devices into the organization's own device-authorization inventory.** A customer's device reaching a hosted application is the customer's compliance problem, addressed through a supply-chain attestation, not something the host inventories as its own authorized device. [f]
- **Writing separate control language for every individual asset in the SSP.** Addressing categories of assets is the expected level; listing controls per device creates redundant text with no added assurance.

## Edge cases

- **Service accounts that live in a shared directory spanning multiple business units.** An account does not need to sit in a dedicated organizational grouping to be in scope; if it exists and touches the CUI environment, it is in scope regardless of where it originated in the directory structure. [b]
- **External customers connecting to a company's own hosted application.** The device-authorization burden for the customer's endpoint sits with the customer, not the host; a signed attestation that the customer will only connect from a compliant device is the accepted approach, rather than technical enforcement on a device the host does not control. [f]
- **Local accounts on domain-joined machines.** These belong in the authorized-user inventory alongside directory accounts; they are not exempt because the machine is otherwise centrally managed. [a]
- **Service accounts tied to infrastructure such as a web server.** A web server process running under its own account needs to appear in the inventory the same way a human account does. [b]
- **Distinguishing this requirement from authentication.** This requirement decides who and what is allowed and then limits access to that list; the authentication requirement verifies identity as a precondition to any access at all. The two are read together but answer different questions.

## Evidence examples

- **Access authorization roster** with name, business justification, and validation date. [a][d]
- **Screenshot of conditional access or access-policy configuration** enforcing the user-limitation objective. [d]
- **Directory group policy or configuration showing logon-rights groups.** [d]
- **Screenshots of service-account permissions** from the directory. [b]
- **Firewall configuration export showing an IP or MAC allowlist.** [c][f]
- **A change or service ticket documenting how a service account was approved.** [b]
- **Baseline documentation listing expected service accounts or automated processes per application.** [b]
- **Network scan output compared against the approved device list.** [c][f]
- **Device connection logs used for periodic compliance checks.** [f]

## Related requirements

- **3.1.2** builds on this one: once users, processes, and devices are identified and limited, 3.1.2 further limits what each is allowed to do once inside.
- **3.5.2** supplies the authentication step this requirement assumes; this requirement decides who is authorized, 3.5.2 verifies the identity claiming that authorization.
- **3.1.5** and **3.1.6** narrow the same authorized-user population by privilege level once this requirement has established who that population is.
- **3.12.1** provides the periodic assessment mechanism that checks whether the enforcement mechanisms named here (allowlists, scans, reviews) are still working.
