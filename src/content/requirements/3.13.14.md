## Requirement

"Control and monitor the use of Voice over Internet Protocol (VoIP) technologies."

## Objectives

[a] use of Voice over Internet Protocol (VoIP) technologies is controlled; and
[b] use of Voice over Internet Protocol (VoIP) technologies is monitored.

## Sufficient

- **Physical or logical (VLAN) separation of voice traffic from the network carrying CUI**, documented as the method of meeting the requirement rather than claimed as a reason the requirement does not apply. [a]
- **A written policy prohibiting CUI discussion over unencrypted voice channels, paired with a documented, approved alternative** (a government-authorized cloud collaboration platform) for when a verbal CUI discussion is genuinely necessary. [a]
- **Not operating any VoIP technology at all is an acceptable state**, so long as the SSP documents that fact instead of marking the requirement not applicable. [a]
- **Naming a specific, limited set of approved VoIP or video-calling technologies as the control answer, and monitoring session traffic to and from only those approved technologies as the monitoring answer.** [a][b]

## Insufficient

- **No defined list of approved VoIP technologies**, so nothing distinguishes sanctioned use from unsanctioned use for an assessor to check against. [a]
- **No monitoring in place for unapproved VoIP technologies or for unapproved use of an otherwise-approved solution.** [b]

## Over-engineering

- **Requiring a government-authorized cloud collaboration platform for every voice call, when CUI is neither being discussed nor shared during most of them.** The rules governing verbal CUI discussion over ordinary phone lines are looser than practitioners sometimes assume, and traditional VoIP that does not carry CUI can often be documented out of the CUI-handling path rather than routed through an authorized platform by default.

## Edge cases

- **Software-based calling applications ("softphones") installed on a workstation that also processes CUI.** Whether that brings the calling application itself into scope for this requirement, distinct from the workstation's other CUI-handling role, is not clearly settled in available discussion; treat it as an open item to confirm.
- **Wi-Fi calling on a personal or company cell phone.** One position argues this does not meaningfully extend network scope, consistent with how voice communication over ordinary telephone service is treated separately from data-in-transit rules; this remains an argument rather than a settled answer, so document the reasoning rather than assume it.
- **A call that originates outside the organization's controlled network and is bridged or forwarded into an in-scope collaboration session.** Whether that bridge undermines the protections of the in-scope session is an open question without a documented resolution; the safer working assumption is that CUI should not be discussed while such a bridge is active.
- **Outsourced VoIP service through a third-party voice provider.** The organization is still expected to control and monitor VoIP use within its own systems, at least to a limited degree, even when a vendor hosts the underlying service, per public procurement guidance; the extent of available monitoring may be limited by what the vendor exposes.
- **Rev 3 direction.** Source discussion notes this specific named requirement may not carry forward in the next revision in its current form, with organizations advised to describe managing VoIP like any other network protocol instead. This is a direction to watch, not the current Rev 2 rule.

## Evidence examples

- **Network diagram showing VoIP traffic physically or logically separated from the CUI-handling network.** [a]
- **Acceptable-use or communications policy prohibiting CUI discussion over uncontrolled voice channels.** [a]
- **List of approved VoIP or collaboration technologies.** [a]
- **Firewall rule set or session traffic monitoring configuration limited to approved technologies.** [b]

## Related requirements

- **3.13.5** (public-facing system separation) and **3.13.1** (boundary protection) share the network-segmentation logic this requirement relies on to keep voice traffic apart from CUI traffic.
- **3.13.13** (mobile code) and **3.13.12** (collaborative device control) are the other technology-specific control-and-monitor requirements in this same part of the family.
