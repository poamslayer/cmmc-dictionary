## Requirement

"Encrypt CUI on mobile devices and mobile computing platforms."

## Objectives

- [a] mobile devices and mobile computing platforms that process, store, or transmit CUI are identified; and
- [b] encryption is employed to protect CUI on identified mobile devices and mobile computing platforms.

## Sufficient

- **Apply the operating system's built-in full-disk encryption to every laptop that handles CUI**, with the encryption keys escrowed in a central endpoint management console as evidence of managed key custody. [a][b]
- **Use container-based application encryption through a mobile application management policy in a government cloud tenant** for phones and tablets, keeping organizational data encrypted within approved applications regardless of device ownership. [b]
- **Maintain a device inventory naming every laptop, phone, and tablet authorized to reach CUI**, kept distinct from the separate connection-control list required elsewhere. [a]
- **Reload any laptop found without full-disk encryption enabled** so the gap is closed rather than argued around. [b]

## Insufficient

- **Leaving a subset of laptops, commonly ones on an alternate operating system, without full-disk encryption enabled** while treating the fleet as covered; every device that can hold CUI needs the protection, not most of them. [b]
- **Arguing a laptop is functionally a desktop because it stays in one building**, without a written policy saying so and without user training on that restriction; the Assessment Guide's own worked example treats laptop encryption as the baseline expectation, and an argument against it is a hard sell without both elements in place. [a][b]

## Over-engineering

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Edge cases

- A device with no path to CUI, no CUI email configured and no CUI-holding application installed, does not need to be secured to the same standard as a device that does; the harder question is whether it still counts as a risk-managed asset because it could connect to the broader network, and the evaluation of that classification is genuinely subjective and depends on whether a real physical or logical separation actually exists.
- Scoping a device out because it only reaches a mail system kept outside the CUI environment depends on being able to show that separation clearly and having the assessor agree with the scoping call before the assessment begins; if the separation cannot be demonstrated, a broader reading that any device able to receive CUI by someone else's mistake must be in scope would sweep in far more than intended, so the practical test is whether the mail system is actually isolated.
- Whether a laptop that never leaves a building can be treated differently than a traveling laptop is unresolved in general; a written no-removal policy backed by user training might carry an assessor, but the outcome depends on the assessor, and it is worth raising directly during assessor selection rather than assuming either way.

## Evidence examples

- Device inventory identifying every mobile device and computing platform authorized to hold CUI. [a]
- Full-disk encryption configuration export with keys shown escrowed in a central management console. [b]
- Application protection policy configuration from a mobile application management platform showing encryption enforced within the managed application. [b]
- Vendor documentation identifying which mobile encryption method is FIPS-validated, cited against the specific devices in the inventory. [b]

## Related requirements

- 3.8.1 and 3.8.2 extend the same CUI media-protection logic to portable media generally.
- 3.13.16 requires confidentiality of CUI at rest more broadly, of which mobile-device encryption is one instance.
- 3.13.11 sets the FIPS-validation bar the encryption method itself must meet.
