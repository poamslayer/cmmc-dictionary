## Requirement

"Protect the authenticity of communications sessions."

## Objectives

[a] the authenticity of communications sessions is protected.

## Sufficient

- **Current-version TLS enforced across devices and specifically verified on VPN connections**, rather than assumed by default because it is common elsewhere in the environment. [a]
- **Certificate renewal tracked and completed before expiration**, so the trust relationship a session depends on does not lapse silently. [a]
- **A written policy naming approved devices, approved browsers, and approved network paths, paired with the technical TLS enforcement**, so the policy and technical layers cover the objective together. [a]
- **For a fully cloud-hosted environment, citing the cloud service provider's own shared-responsibility documentation for the platform's side of session protection**, with the organization's documented responsibility limited to items such as using an approved, current browser. [a]

## Insufficient

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Over-engineering

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Edge cases

- **Non-browser communication channels, such as VPN tunnels or secure file transfer, do not always default to an authenticated, protected session the way browser traffic increasingly does.** Each channel needs its own verification rather than assuming one enforced setting covers every path.
- **For a shared cloud environment, exactly where the provider's responsibility ends and the organization's documented responsibility begins should be confirmed against that provider's own shared-responsibility documentation rather than assumed.**

## Evidence examples

- **Configuration showing current-version TLS enforced on managed devices and tested on VPN connections.** [a]
- **Certificate inventory or renewal tracking showing valid, non-expired certificates for the organization's own services.** [a]
- **Written policy naming approved devices, browsers, and network paths.** [a]

## Related requirements

- **3.13.8** (data in transit) often shares the same TLS configuration as this requirement, since a session needs both confidentiality and a verified, unchanging identity on each end.
- **3.13.11** (CUI Encryption) supplies the FIPS-validation standard for the cryptographic mechanisms this requirement's session protection may rely on.
