## Requirement

"Require multifactor authentication to establish nonlocal maintenance sessions via external network connections and terminate such connections when nonlocal maintenance is complete."

## Objectives

[a] multifactor authentication is used to establish nonlocal maintenance sessions via external network connections; and
[b] nonlocal maintenance sessions established via external network connections are terminated when nonlocal maintenance is complete.

## Sufficient

- **A screen-sharing session where the organization's own user drives the interaction and controls what happens, rather than granting the outside party direct access to the system, is generally treated differently from nonlocal maintenance requiring multifactor authentication under this requirement.** The organization retains control of the session rather than ceding direct access. [a]
- **Where an external service provider maintains a persistent management connection into the environment and that provider is treated as part of the system's authorization boundary, the multifactor and termination objectives are addressed through the boundary's own access controls, including whatever mechanism that platform uses to end individual user sessions.** [a][b]
- **A responsibility matrix or similar written confirmation from an external service provider stating that the provider applies multifactor authentication to its own nonlocal maintenance sessions gives an assessor a documented basis for objective [a] without the organization independently instrumenting the provider's side of the connection.** [a]

## Insufficient

- **A remote maintenance session authenticated with only a single factor, such as a password alone, without a second factor.** This does not meet objective [a] regardless of how strong the password policy is, because the objective specifically calls for multifactor authentication for this session type. [a]
- **A maintenance connection that persists indefinitely after the maintenance activity has finished, with no manual or automatic step that closes it out.** This does not meet the termination objective even if the session was originally established correctly. [b]

## Over-engineering

- **Building a fully separate, dedicated remote-access platform solely for nonlocal maintenance, distinct from the remote access mechanism already used and controlled under the organization's other remote-access requirements.** This adds infrastructure the objectives do not require when the existing mechanism already supports multifactor authentication and session termination. [a][b]

## Edge cases

- **Whether a persistent connection maintained by a management platform ever truly "completes" in a way that satisfies the termination objective is genuinely disputed.** One position treats the underlying device connection as continuous by design and focuses instead on terminating the individual user's session as the practical unit that can be closed out; a stricter reading would want the device-level connection itself interrupted after each maintenance action; the outcome depends on the assessor.
- **What activity counts as nonlocal maintenance via external network connections is also read differently case to case.** One framing limits it to an outside party remoting in to manage equipment such as operational technology, storage, or network gear, using a connection method outside the organization's normal way of connecting to its own systems; a broader framing would capture any remote troubleshooting session regardless of who initiates it or what tool is used. The narrower framing is the more commonly applied one.

## Evidence examples

- **Multifactor authentication configuration on the remote maintenance access mechanism.** [a]
- **Session timeout or termination settings on remote access or management tooling.** [b]
- **Written confirmation from an external service provider describing its own multifactor authentication practice for nonlocal maintenance.** [a]
- **Authorization boundary documentation addressing whether an external provider's management platform sits inside or outside the boundary.** [a][b]
- **Session termination or logout records from remote maintenance tooling.** [b]

## Related requirements

- **3.1.12** and **3.1.14** govern the control of remote access sessions and the access points allowed for them. **3.1.13** and **3.1.15** add cryptographic protection and privileged-command authorization for those same sessions. Together they set the general remote-access baseline; this requirement layers a multifactor authentication requirement specific to maintenance sessions on top of that baseline.
- **3.5.3** supplies the multifactor authentication baseline this requirement extends to the maintenance use case specifically.
- **3.7.2** controls over maintenance mechanisms overlap with how an external provider's persistent connection is treated for both requirements.
