## Requirement

"Ensure equipment removed for off-site maintenance is sanitized of any CUI."

## Objectives

[a] equipment to be removed from organizational spaces for off-site maintenance is sanitized of any CUI.

## Sufficient

- **Sanitization of the actual storage media, not a logical access restriction layered on top of it, is what the objective calls for.** A laptop being sent out for repair is equipment within scope even though it is a general-purpose device rather than dedicated storage hardware. [a]
- **Where a device is managed through a cloud-based device management platform, an enrollment reset that wipes the device back to a clean state before it leaves organizational control satisfies the sanitization objective for that device.** [a]
- **The sanitization approach should match published federal guidance for the type of media involved: clearing, purging, or destroying, depending on what is feasible and what the media is.** Matching the method to the media type, rather than defaulting to the most extreme option every time, still reaches a MET finding. [a]

## Insufficient

- **A logical access control such as multifactor authentication placed on a device does not substitute for sanitizing it.** Whoever performs off-site maintenance typically has access to the full storage medium regardless of any per-account access restriction, so the underlying data remains exposed unless it is actually sanitized. [a]
- **Describing a logical access restriction as an alternate implementation of a physical sanitization requirement is not an accepted substitute.** An organization may document a genuinely different but equivalent way of meeting the objective, but a control that leaves CUI physically present and recoverable does not reach the objective by another name. [a]

## Over-engineering

- **Destroying media entirely, for equipment that will be returned to service after routine repair, when clearing or purging following published sanitization guidance would already make the data infeasible to recover.** Destruction is appropriate when the media will not be reused, but it spends more than the objective requires when the equipment is coming back into service. [a]

## Edge cases

- **Whether devices outside the traditional laptop or server category, such as personally owned mobile devices used under a bring-your-own-device policy, or peripheral equipment like copiers and scanners, fall within "equipment" for this requirement turns on whether they can hold or process CUI.** Broader system-component readings used elsewhere in the requirement set, and this requirement's own reach to any system component, support treating equipment broadly rather than narrowly.
- **Whether a technician who repairs a remote employee's device in front of that employee, at the employee's own location, counts as off-site maintenance is unsettled.** The more relevant question is usually whether the employee has the standing to actually supervise what the technician does, which ties the scenario to the personnel-supervision requirement rather than resolving cleanly here.

## Evidence examples

- **Documented sanitization procedure specifying the method used before equipment leaves organizational control.** [a]
- **Device management platform reset or wipe logs for cloud-managed endpoints.** [a]
- **Sanitization or destruction records for storage media removed from service.** [a]
- **Policy prohibiting equipment from leaving organizational spaces for maintenance before sanitization is confirmed.** [a]

## Related requirements

- **3.7.1** and **3.7.2** govern the broader maintenance activity and its controls; this requirement is the specific safeguard that applies when equipment physically leaves organizational control.
- **3.8.3** governs media sanitization more broadly, before disposal or reuse, and shares the same underlying sanitization guidance this requirement draws on for equipment headed off-site.
