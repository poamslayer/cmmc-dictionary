## Requirement

"Control access to media containing CUI and maintain accountability for media during transport outside of controlled areas."

## Objectives

[a] access to media containing CUI is controlled; and
[b] accountability for media containing CUI is maintained during transport outside of controlled areas.

## Sufficient

- **Laptops issued to hybrid or remote employees who regularly take them home**, provided the organization can describe how it handles portable media generally rather than treating the laptop as an unmanaged exception. [a][b]
- **Encrypting everything that travels, and falling back on physical protections when encryption is not available**, such as opaque or double wrapping, a sanitized shipping address, and locked containers, echoing the handling practices used for higher-sensitivity material. For international travel, a signed travel authorization, an IT ticket documenting the trip, and enabled remote access round out the process. [b]
- **Authorized use of CUI-bearing removable media at an employee's home**, as long as company policy limits who may use it and the organization accounts for its use once it is outside the controlled space, rather than treating home use as automatically out of scope. [a][b]
- **Encryption at rest, particularly when company-owned portable storage is restricted to company-managed workstations.** Practitioner consensus treats this as the single most effective way to reduce risk for remote-work scenarios, ahead of procedural controls alone. [b]

## Edge cases

- **Traveling by air with paper CUI or a prototype in a carry-on case.** Practitioner consensus treats the item as personal carry-on that must remain with the traveler at all times and should not be checked. If an inspecting agency needs to examine it, requesting a private screening area is reasonable; a routine security inspection under a lawful government purpose is generally acceptable, since the concern is exposure to the general public rather than the inspecting agency itself. Material carrying special dissemination controls needs the inspecting party's eligibility confirmed first.
- **Whether a physical prototype itself counts as CUI.** Practitioner consensus holds that physical items generally are not CUI, because CUI is information rather than an object; the technical data a prototype embodies is what may be controlled, which affects how the prototype itself should be handled during transport.
- **Overlap with the alternate work site requirement.** Whether an employee may take CUI home on removable media, and whether a home qualifies as a controlled area, are treated as alternate-work-site questions rather than pure accountability questions. Secure storage and access control while the media is at home fall under the physical-protection requirements; this requirement's accountability obligation applies specifically while the media is actually in transit outside any controlled area.
- **Transporting backup media to offsite storage.** Encrypting the media is treated as the preferred, simplest way to reduce both transport and storage concerns for backup tapes and similar bulk media.

## Evidence examples

- **Media transport procedure** describing how CUI-bearing media is handled outside controlled areas. [a][b]
- **Transport log** recording movement of specific media items. [b]
- **Encryption configuration** for portable storage devices used in transport. [b]
- **Travel authorization record** for CUI carried on domestic or international travel. [b]
- **Alternate work site policy** addressing CUI-bearing media used or stored at home. [a][b]
- **Locked container or physical protection procedure** for media that cannot be encrypted. [b]

## Related requirements

- **3.8.1** and **3.8.2** govern physical control and access while the media sits inside a controlled area, on either side of the transport this requirement addresses.
