## Requirement

"Track, review, approve or disapprove, and log changes to organizational systems."

## Objectives

[a] changes to the system are tracked;
[b] changes to the system are reviewed;
[c] changes to the system are approved or disapproved; and
[d] changes to the system are logged.

## Sufficient

- **A change process scaled to a very small organization: the technical staff member proposes a change, the whole team reviews it together with a stated justification, and the meeting date, attendees, and outcome go into a simple log (a spreadsheet is accepted).** A change record of this kind, describing what changed, on what platform, by whom, when, whether testing occurred, who approved or disapproved it, and the assessed security impact, has passed a self-assessment review at that scale. [a][b][c][d]
- **A tiered review process where not every change goes through the full change board ahead of time; minor changes are simply logged and rolled into a periodic report the board can query.** This tiering is acceptable as long as it is written into policy or the system security plan rather than applied informally. [a][b][d]
- **For temporary or troubleshooting changes, logging the change wherever is convenient, including as a note on the related trouble ticket, as long as it is written down somewhere.** [a][d]
- **A defined emergency-change allowance: a written statement or procedure naming who can make an emergency change and where it gets logged, with approval following after the fact rather than before.** [a][c][d]

## Insufficient

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Over-engineering

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Edge cases

- **Whether granting a user a new permission (a folder or a collaboration-site access grant, for example) counts as a change under this requirement.** One position treats any permission grant that falls outside a user's normal role as a change requiring the full track-review-approve-log cycle; the risk-based refinement of that position treats more of the same kind of access as low concern and a genuinely new kind of access, especially anything touching separation of duties, as needing a closer look. Assessors generally expect the review-and-log cycle for access grants; how strictly the risk-based line gets drawn is where the outcome depends on the assessor.
- **A very small organization where the person proposing a change is also the only person technically capable of reviewing it.** Rather than treating this as unsatisfiable, the accepted practice includes non-technical staff in the review meeting to discuss the justification and impact, since the review is meant to confirm the need and the impact were considered, not to duplicate the technical judgment of the one person qualified to make it.
- **What counts as a "change" in the first place.** The 800-171 version of this requirement dropped the earlier framing (found in its NIST SP 800-53 origin) that had organizations define which types of changes require change control, leaving a gap in guardrails; the practical response is to treat this as broader than purely technical changes and include things like access-permission changes, not just configuration or software changes.

## Evidence examples

- **Change log spreadsheet** recording meeting date, attendees, description of the change, affected platform, who made it, who approved or disapproved it, whether testing was required, and an assessed security-impact rating. [a][b][c][d]
- **Trouble ticket entry** logging a change as part of an incident or service record. [a][d]
- **Periodic change digest or report** summarizing minor changes for the review body. [b][d]
- **Change control board meeting minutes** showing what was reviewed and the disposition of each item. [b][c]

## Related requirements
