## Requirement

"Develop and implement plans of action designed to correct deficiencies and reduce or eliminate vulnerabilities in organizational systems."

## Objectives

[a] deficiencies and vulnerabilities to be addressed by the plan of action are identified;
[b] a plan of action is developed to correct identified deficiencies and reduce or eliminate identified vulnerabilities; and
[c] the plan of action is implemented to correct identified deficiencies and reduce or eliminate identified vulnerabilities.

## Sufficient

- **A ticket-based POA&M tracker, in any ticketing system, carrying the essential fields: the gap identified, who owns it, when it opened, and when it closed.** Practitioner consensus accepts this format so long as those fields exist and are kept current. [a][b][c]
- **A spreadsheet with separate tabs for the POA&M and the OPA.** The form does not matter; what matters is that the content and intent of each tracked item is clear and it lands in the right tab. [a][b][c]
- **A workflow where discovered gaps are logged as tickets and those tickets feed a POA&M tracker**, giving a documented, traceable path from discovery to resolution. [a][b][c]
- **Treating issues surfaced during normal incident response, vulnerability scanning, or routine monitoring as business as usual**, addressed through those existing documented procedures rather than always requiring a new POA&M item. [c]
- **Fixing a single, isolated mistake directly, without opening a POA&M item for it.** One instance of a control slipping on a single asset is treated as an operational correction, not a deficiency requiring a documented plan of action. [c]

## Insufficient

- **Logging process improvements, such as migrating tracking tools, as POA&M items.** Mixing genuine security gaps with unrelated workflow changes risks confusing an assessor about the organization's actual compliance status and blurs items that carry a hard remediation clock with those that do not. [a][b]
- **Treating the POA&M and the OPA as interchangeable**, or using either term loosely without regard to which one actually applies to a given item. Confusing the two creates real risk, because they carry different obligations. [a][b][c]
- **Leaving a discovered, fixable issue undocumented because it resembles an OPA condition.** An issue the organization can actually resolve, with an assignable owner and completion date, belongs on the POA&M, not treated as though it is outside the organization's control. [a][b][c]

## Over-engineering

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Edge cases

- **Distinguishing the POA&M from the OPA.** Per 32 CFR 170.4 and 170.21, the POA&M is the document that sets resources, milestones, and a scheduled completion date; the OPA covers temporary or enduring conditions the organization does not control and cannot put a completion date against, such as waiting on a vendor patch or a permanent, documented exception. The distinguishing question is whether the organization controls the fix and can assign it a date; presence of a milestone is what separates the two mechanisms.
- **Whether an item on the POA&M still counts as compliant under a different framework than under CMMC.** Practitioner discussion notes that DFARS 7012 tolerates a documented gap on a POA&M as compliant, while CMMC scoring reads an open POA&M item as the associated requirement being not currently met.
- **At what point a single missed configuration on one asset among many becomes a POA&M item rather than a one-off fix.** The accepted threshold is that an isolated instance is simply corrected; the same gap recurring as a pattern warrants a POA&M item so the organization can investigate and address the root cause. This determination is inherently judgment-based, so the outcome depends on the assessor when the pattern is ambiguous.

## Evidence examples

- **POA&M tracker entries or tickets showing the deficiency, owner, open date, and close date.** [a][b][c]
- **A tracking spreadsheet or system with items correctly separated between the POA&M tab and the OPA tab.** [a][b][c]
- **Records of routine incident response, vulnerability assessment, or monitoring activity conducted per documented procedure**, showing issues were addressed through normal operations where appropriate. [c]
- **Closed POA&M items with a documented resolution**, demonstrating the plan was carried through to completion rather than only opened. [c]

## Related requirements

- **3.12.1** typically supplies the findings that populate this requirement's tracked plans, since periodic effectiveness assessment is a common source of identified deficiencies.
- **3.12.3** surfaces ongoing findings the same way, feeding items into this requirement's tracking mechanism as they are discovered between periodic reviews.
- **3.11.1** provides the risk assessment output that frequently identifies the vulnerabilities this requirement's plans are built to reduce or eliminate.
- **3.12.4** documents, in the system security plan, the requirements this requirement's tracked items are correcting gaps against.
