## Requirement

"Periodically assess the security controls in organizational systems to determine if the controls are effective in their application."

## Objectives

[a] the frequency of security control assessments is defined; and
[b] security controls are assessed with the defined frequency to determine if the controls are effective in their application.

## Sufficient

- **An annual self-assessment of the full requirement set, at minimum.** The Assessment Guide's own consideration ties "periodically" to at least once a year, and practitioner consensus treats annual as the accepted floor. [a][b]
- **A risk-based schedule that assesses higher-priority controls more often than once a year**, with lower-risk controls still covered on an annual baseline. Weekly, monthly, and quarterly cadences for specific controls, layered over an annual review of everything, are accepted as exceeding the minimum. [a][b]
- **An explicit, written frequency**, stated in policy rather than left as an undefined "periodic." [a]
- **An assessment that asks whether each control is actually achieving its intended effect**, not only whether the organization is doing what its plan says. Practitioner consensus draws a sharp line between a compliance read-through of the system security plan and a genuine effectiveness review; the latter is what this requirement expects. [b]

## Insufficient

- **A compliance-only self-check that simply re-reads the system security plan and confirms the described activity is happening.** This answers "am I doing this" rather than "is this an effective way to meet the intent," and practitioner consensus treats the compliance-only version as falling short of the objective. [b]
- **Planning to validate a control only if and when a real incident occurs, with no structured review built in.** A stated intention to "test it when something happens" does not itself constitute the periodic assessment this requirement calls for. [b]
- **Leaving the assessment frequency unstated or implicit.** The first objective requires a defined frequency before the second objective, which measures the assessment carried out against that frequency, can be met at all. [a]

## Over-engineering

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Edge cases

- **Distinguishing this requirement from 3.12.3.** This requirement is a periodic, point-in-time effectiveness review, typically annual or on a defined schedule; 3.12.3 is the ongoing, ambient monitoring that runs between those periodic reviews. Practitioner discussion summarizes the distinction as periodic versus ongoing, and the two are commonly implemented together rather than as substitutes for each other.
- **Whether an actual security incident can substitute for a planned assessment exercise.** One position holds that a real incident cannot stand in for a structured test of the plan; another position accepts a real incident as a substitute when the organization's lessons-learned documentation from that incident is as thorough as what a formal exercise would have produced. The outcome depends on the assessor.
- **How often a triennial statutory assessment cycle applies.** A three-year cycle exists in the CMMC framework, but it applies only to a narrow set of contracts carrying a specific self-assessment requirement, not to the general population of companies pursuing Level 2 certification; for nearly all organizations the operative floor remains the Assessment Guide's "at least annually" standard.

## Evidence examples

- **Written policy stating the defined assessment frequency.** [a]
- **Assessment records or reports from each periodic review, dated to the defined interval.** [a][b]
- **Documented findings that evaluate whether a control is achieving its intended effect**, not just whether the activity described in the system security plan occurred. [b]
- **A risk-based assessment calendar** showing higher-frequency review of higher-priority controls alongside the annual baseline. [a][b]
- **Lessons-learned records from either a planned exercise or an actual incident**, used to demonstrate the review was substantive. [b]

## Related requirements

- **3.12.3** carries the ongoing monitoring that runs between this requirement's periodic reviews; the two work together to cover both point-in-time effectiveness and continuous awareness.
- **3.12.2** receives the deficiencies this requirement's assessments identify, turning findings into a tracked plan of action.
- **3.11.1** supplies the risk context that a risk-based assessment schedule under this requirement typically draws on when deciding which controls need more frequent review.
