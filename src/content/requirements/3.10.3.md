## Requirement

"Escort visitors and monitor visitor activity."

## Objectives

[a] visitors are escorted; and
[b] visitor activity is monitored.

## Sufficient

- **Personnel who need regular, recurring facility access documented as third-party personnel rather than visitors**, added to a facility access list marked contractor, tenant, or vendor. The escort obligation attaches to visitors, not to this population. [a]
- **Individuals holding a permanent physical access authorization credential are outside this requirement entirely.** Assessment Guide discussion. They are governed by whatever access-list and screening controls apply to authorized personnel generally, not by the escort-and-monitor objective. [a]
- **Cleaning and janitorial staff treated as vetted third-party personnel** rather than visitors, provided either they cannot reach CUI or they receive the same baseline CUI-handling awareness given to employees. [a][b]
- **For a small organization where personnel recognize each other on sight, no fixed headcount forces a badge program.** The test is whether the organization can show how it limits access, including training on spotting someone who doesn't belong and a documented escort process for anyone who does need to be let in. [a][b]
- **The person doing the escorting is itself the monitoring**, with an entrance camera or a logged badge event as useful support rather than a separate requirement. [b]
- **A visible visitor indicator, such as a lanyard badge, giving a fast visual cue that separates a visitor from authorized personnel.** [b]

## Insufficient

- **A cleaning contractor let into the building outside business hours while no organizational staff are around, with nothing in place that would stop CUI exposure**, such as locked storage, encrypted devices, or screen locks. That access window leaves both objectives unmet. [a][b]
- **A third-party vendor whose staffing company will not identify the individual workers it sends.** Without knowing who is actually on site, the organization cannot authorize them as known third-party personnel, and the default reverts to needing an escort. [a]

## Over-engineering

- **No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.**

## Edge cases

- **A cleared individual with an active, formal visit-authorization record on file, used elsewhere for classified-visit programs, has sometimes been treated as authorized personnel rather than a visitor**, using a no-escort or temporary-access credential instead of a visitor badge. This depends on the organization already running that kind of visit-authorization process for other reasons, so the outcome depends on the assessor.
- **A corporate employee outside the CUI-handling group who is physically present in a building where CUI work happens, but cannot reach CUI systems** because offices are locked and labs are badge-controlled, is generally resolved by authorizing and vetting them as in-scope personnel rather than escorting them as a visitor on every visit.
- **Other contractors or subcontractors supporting the same program, each carrying their own separate compliance obligations**, can be identified and treated as authorized once the organization documents how it verifies their status, rather than escorting them as visitors each time.

## Evidence examples

- **A facility access list marking non-employee personnel as contractor, tenant, or vendor rather than visitor.** [a]
- **A visitor sign-in and sign-out log**, whether a logbook or a lanyard-badge-based system. [b]
- **A training record on identifying and handling an unrecognized individual.** [a][b]
- **Camera footage at entrance points supporting visitor time tracking.** [b]
- **A policy defining what makes someone "authorized personnel" rather than a "visitor."** [a]

## Related requirements

- **3.10.1** sets the authorized-individuals population this requirement's visitor definition sits outside of.
- **3.10.4** is satisfied in part by the same visitor sign-in and sign-out record or camera footage this requirement produces.
- **3.9.1** governs the screening that lets someone move from being escorted as a visitor to being treated as authorized personnel.
