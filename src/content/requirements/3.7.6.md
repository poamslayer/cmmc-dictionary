## Requirement

"Supervise the maintenance activities of maintenance personnel without required access authorization."

## Objectives

[a] maintenance personnel without required access authorization are supervised during maintenance activities.

## Sufficient

- **Anyone performing maintenance-type work without a standing access authorization for CUI, whether a software vendor's technician or a facility trade such as HVAC, plumbing, or fire-system service, is treated as a visitor requiring monitoring and escort while on organizational premises.** [a]
- **Issuing a temporary, time-limited credential scoped to only the systems that need work, paired with monitoring of the individual's physical and network activity for the duration of the visit, and revoking that credential when the work is done.** This is an accepted way to supervise personnel who lack standing authorization from start to finish. [a]

## Insufficient

- **Allowing maintenance personnel without standing access authorization to work unaccompanied on the assumption that a signed visitor log alone is sufficient.** This does not meet the objective; the requirement calls for the activity itself to be supervised while it is happening, not only recorded after the fact. [a]

## Over-engineering

- **Running a full background screening process on every short-notice maintenance visitor before allowing supervised work to begin.** The objective is satisfied by supervision during the activity itself; screening every occasional visitor at the depth applied to a standing employee adds cost the requirement does not call for when escort and monitoring are already in place. [a]

## Edge cases

- **This requirement applies specifically to personnel lacking required access authorization, which reaches beyond information technology vendors to cover any maintenance trade with physical access to spaces containing organizational systems.** It is common in practice to find networking equipment located in mechanical or utility spaces those trades service, which brings this requirement into play even for non-technical maintenance visits.
- **There is overlap between supervising maintenance personnel under this requirement and the physical-access escort expectations that govern the operational environment generally.** Where a facility trade's work brings them into a physically protected space, both point toward the same escort-and-monitor practice, and a single escort procedure can typically satisfy both rather than requiring two separate ones.

## Evidence examples

- **Visitor escort and sign-in and sign-out log for maintenance personnel without standing access authorization.** [a]
- **Temporary credential issuance and expiration record for maintenance access.** [a]
- **Written policy requiring supervision of maintenance personnel who lack access authorization.** [a]
- **List distinguishing personnel with standing access authorization from those without it.** [a]
- **Training record for the employee or escort assigned to supervise a given maintenance visit.** [a]

## Related requirements

- **3.10.1** physical access control for the operational environment overlaps with this requirement whenever unauthorized maintenance personnel are physically present in a space housing organizational systems.
- **3.7.2** personnel control over maintenance generally; this requirement is the specific case of personnel who lack standing authorization.
- **3.9.2** personnel screening informs which maintenance personnel already hold standing authorization versus which require supervision under this requirement.
