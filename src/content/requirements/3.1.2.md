## Requirement

"Limit information system access to the types of transactions and functions that authorized users are permitted to execute."

## Objectives

[a] the types of transactions and functions that authorized users are permitted to execute are defined; and
[b] system access is limited to the defined types of transactions and functions for authorized users.

## Sufficient

- **A matrix documenting user types and the functions each type may perform** (guest, standard user, administrator, human resources, and similar categories) has been accepted for objective [a]. [a]
- **Built-in cloud platform roles used for role-based access control** are an accepted mechanism for objective [b], though a fuller account of what each role actually grants strengthens the evidence beyond a bare role-assignment screenshot. [b]
- **In very small organizations without a directory service**, distinguishing a local administrator account from a standard account, combined with application allowlisting and confining CUI access to specific approved applications, has been accepted as adequate. [a][b]
- **Role-based restriction to only the applications and systems a job function requires** (for example, excluding engineering staff from human resources systems, or excluding staff who do not need it from accounting software) satisfies the intent. [a][b]
- **Defining permitted functions along create, read, update, and delete lines**, as the Assessment Guide itself frames the objective, is an accepted way to state the objective [a] definition. [a]

## Insufficient

- **A personally enabled device model that allows unrestricted personal software installation on the in-scope device**, without a companion policy tying that allowance back to least-functionality controls, undermines the defined-and-enforced structure this requirement expects. [a][b]
- **Permitting personal use of a device without stating, anywhere in policy, that personal connections and applications may not be used for official duties or touch official data.** This leaves objective [a] undefined in practice. [a]

## Over-engineering

- **Standing up a directory service purely to satisfy this requirement in a small organization.** Local account types plus application-level controls and a written definition of permitted functions meet the same objectives at far lower cost.
- **Banning all personal use of the in-scope system.** Ordinary personal browsing or streaming is treated as normal on systems that also handle CUI; the requirement is about defining and enforcing limits, not eliminating everything that is not work.

## Edge cases

- **Personal-use allowances that mix with official duties.** A corporate-owned, personally enabled arrangement is defensible, but it pulls in several other requirements at once: it needs a denylist of prohibited software, a rule that personal connections cannot touch official data, monitoring to identify what is actually connecting, and it complicates the least-functionality requirement, the external-connections requirement, and the mobile-code requirement. Whether the added complexity is worth the convenience is an organizational risk decision, best documented with management sign-off.
- **Browser-based personal applications instead of installed apps.** Using a web version of a personal application does not resolve the underlying question of whether the connection is identified and controlled; it only moves the same analysis to a different requirement.

## Evidence examples

- **User type-to-function matrix** listing account types and the functions each may execute. [a]
- **Configuration export or screenshot showing the technical enforcement of a role's limits.** [b]
- **Role assignment screenshots from a cloud identity platform**, ideally paired with the role definitions behind them. [b]
- **Application control tool configuration** showing per-user-type restrictions. [b]
- **Access control policy language covering personal-use provisions**, where a personal-use allowance exists. [a]
- **A denylist of prohibited applications or connections.** [a]

## Related requirements

- **3.1.1** identifies who is authorized before this requirement limits what they may do once inside.
- **3.1.5** narrows the same access model further by separating privileged from non-privileged functions.
- **3.4.7** (least functionality) is directly implicated whenever a personal-use allowance is part of the answer here.
- **3.1.20** and **3.13.13** pick up the external-connection and mobile-code questions a personal-use policy leaves open.
