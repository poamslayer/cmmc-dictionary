## Requirement

"Monitor security controls on an ongoing basis to ensure the continued effectiveness of the controls."

## Objectives

[a] security controls are monitored on an ongoing basis to ensure the continued effectiveness of those controls.

## Sufficient

- **Manual, spreadsheet-based monitoring in smaller organizations**, tracking pass or fail status, reviewer comments, and the date each check occurred. Practitioner consensus accepts a manual approach as long as the organization can produce records proving the monitoring is actually happening, not only that a plan exists to do it. [a]
- **Recurring tasks in an existing project or ticketing tool**, closed out with notes each time, treated as strong evidence because the completed record shows both that the check happened and what was found. [a]
- **Combining related requirements, such as risk assessment activity, periodic security control assessment, and incident response follow-up, into a single ongoing monitoring narrative.** Practitioner consensus accepts this layered approach as satisfying the "ongoing" expectation without a dedicated, separate monitoring program. [a]
- **Monitoring the highest-priority controls on a defined recurring cadence while relying on the annual review under the periodic-assessment requirement to eventually touch everything else.** This is accepted as satisfying the requirement, though touching every control within the ongoing process itself is described as the stronger, lower-risk version of the same approach. [a]
- **Treating "ongoing" as an organization-defined value the company itself sets and documents**, rather than a fixed frequency imposed from outside. [a]
- **Adapting a continuous-monitoring framework built for federal cloud authorizations, prioritizing frequent checks on the most volatile items** such as patch status, asset inventory accuracy, and documentation drift. Assessment Guide discussion. [a]

## Insufficient

- **A policy or system security plan that states monitoring will occur, with no records showing it actually occurred.** A stated intention without dated, retained evidence of execution does not answer what an assessor wants to see. [a]
- **Selecting a handful of favored controls to watch closely while leaving the rest of the requirement set effectively unmonitored**, with no path for those other controls to ever be touched by the ongoing process. [a]
- **Monitoring records with no date attached.** Without a date field, a record cannot establish that the monitoring actually happened on an ongoing basis rather than once, after the fact. [a]

## Over-engineering

- **Building a fully automated, all-320-objective monitoring calendar with individually tuned frequencies for every objective, layered with automated scheduling and spot-check thresholds.** Practitioner discussion acknowledges this kind of build is effective and even described as helpful for staying organized, but it is also described as a substantial standalone project; a risk-based subset of high-priority controls monitored on a defined cadence, backed by an annual full review, satisfies the objective at a fraction of the build effort.

## Edge cases

- **Distinguishing this requirement from 3.12.1.** The Assessment Guide expects this requirement's monitoring to occur more frequently than the periodic assessment described in 3.12.1; practitioner discussion notes the two read similarly on the surface, with periodic versus ongoing frequency being the operative distinction between them.
- **Whether monitoring only the highest-priority controls, with everything else caught by an annual assessment, is enough versus monitoring every control within the ongoing process itself.** One position treats monitoring all controls as implied by the requirement even though a narrower approach can still earn a met finding; another treats hitting every control within the ongoing process as the materially stronger approach that reduces residual risk. The outcome depends on the assessor.
- **Whether automation is required.** No position requires automated tooling; a manual process with dated, retained records is treated as sufficient, though automation supports more frequent and more consistent checks at scale.

## Evidence examples

- **A monitoring spreadsheet with columns for the control, pass or fail status, reviewer comments, date reviewed, and who performed the review.** [a]
- **Completed recurring tickets with notes documenting what was checked and found during each monitoring cycle.** [a]
- **A continuous-monitoring checklist mapping monitored activities to specific requirements with an assigned frequency for each.** [a]
- **Records showing risk assessment, periodic security control assessment, and incident response follow-up activity feeding into the ongoing monitoring narrative.** [a]
- **Dashboards or reports demonstrating that monitoring activity is occurring and being reviewed by responsible staff.** [a]

## Related requirements

- **3.12.1** supplies the periodic, point-in-time effectiveness review that this requirement's ongoing monitoring runs between and complements.
- **3.12.2** receives the deficiencies this requirement's ongoing monitoring surfaces, turning findings into tracked corrective action.
- **3.11.1** contributes risk assessment output that frequently informs which controls this requirement's monitoring prioritizes for more frequent review.
- **3.12.4** documents, in the system security plan, the monitoring approach and frequency this requirement establishes.
