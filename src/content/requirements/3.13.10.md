## Requirement

"Establish and manage cryptographic keys for cryptography employed in organizational systems."

## Objectives

[a] cryptographic keys are established whenever cryptography is employed; and
[b] cryptographic keys are managed whenever cryptography is employed.

## Sufficient

- **Treating this requirement as governing key management the organization itself performs**, such as an internal certificate authority or keys generated for encrypted backups, distinct from key management a cloud or software-as-a-service provider performs on the organization's behalf. [a][b]
- **Tracking keys issued by an internal certificate authority within that system, and tracking keys issued by an external certificate authority through a defined IT responsibility for renewal and security**, documented as two parallel, named processes rather than left implicit. [a][b]
- **For fully cloud-hosted CUI storage and collaboration, citing the cloud provider's own shared-responsibility documentation** to show the provider manages the underlying keys for that specific service. [b]
- **Storing a disk-encryption recovery key in a location separated by access control from the encrypted data itself**, so that a single compromised administrative account cannot reach both the key and the data it protects. [b]

## Insufficient

- **Storing the recovery or encryption key for a backup in the same location as the backup data it protects.** This is a frequently observed failure that collapses the separation the objective is testing for. [b]
- **Any arrangement where a single compromised account can reach both the encrypted data and the key that protects it.** That single point of failure defeats the purpose of managing the key separately from the data. [b]

## Over-engineering

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Edge cases

- **Browser-based upload or download of CUI to a government-authorized cloud tenant.** The key management for that session's encryption is the cloud provider's responsibility under its own shared-responsibility documentation, distinct from keys the organization directly generates and manages, such as disk-encryption keys, even when both ultimately rely on FIPS-validated modules.
- **A device configured with its operating system in FIPS mode is a separate fact from who manages the specific key used for a given cryptographic operation.** Having FIPS mode enabled does not by itself answer the key-management question for that operation; the two objectives should be documented separately.

## Evidence examples

- **Written description in policy or SSP language distinguishing internally issued and externally issued certificate or key tracking.** [a][b]
- **Cloud provider shared-responsibility documentation identifying the provider as responsible for key management for a specific service.** [b]
- **Access control configuration showing the recovery-key location and the encrypted-data location have non-overlapping administrative membership.** [b]
- **Inventory associating each generated disk-encryption key with its device, with access to that inventory restricted to authorized administrators.** [a][b]

## Related requirements

- **3.13.11** (CUI Encryption) names the FIPS-validation standard that keys established and managed under this requirement ultimately support.
- **3.1.19** (mobile device encryption) depends on this requirement, since an encrypted mobile device is only as protected as the key management behind its encryption.
- **3.13.8** (data in transit) and 3.8.6 (media encryption) both rely on cryptographic mechanisms whose keys this requirement's establishment and management practices cover.
