## Requirement

"Prevent reuse of identifiers for a defined period."

## Objectives

[a] a period within which identifiers cannot be reused is defined; and
[b] reuse of identifiers is prevented within the defined period.

## Sufficient

- **A fixed reuse-prevention window, commonly around two years, before a username or email address can be reassigned to a different person.** [a][b]
- **Moving departed-user accounts into a disabled-accounts group within the directory and never deleting them.** The identifier stays reserved and cannot be reissued to someone else, which meets the objective without a separate reuse-tracking mechanism. [b]
- **A documented reuse period in policy, followed procedurally, for organizations without an on-premises directory to enforce it technically.** [a][b]
- **Allowing the same individual to receive their own prior identifier back**, for example a returning employee, when the exception is written into policy or procedure and reflected in the System Security Plan. The objective is about preventing reassignment to a different person, not about the same person reusing their own identifier. [a][b]

## Insufficient

- **Reassigning a former employee's username or account identifier to a new, different hire before the defined period has elapsed**, whether or not the reuse was accidental. [b]
- **Having no defined reuse period at all**, even where identifiers happen not to be reused in practice; the period itself has to be defined and documented, not merely avoided by chance. [a]

## Over-engineering

- **Splitting a person's login name from their email address to obscure the naming pattern.** This is a security-enhancement practice from a related access-management control family that was not carried into this requirement; the platform vendor's own guidance for cloud identity generally recommends keeping the two aligned rather than split.
- **Extending identifier-reuse prevention logic to device identifiers.** Device identifiers are treated differently than person identifiers in the broader control family this requirement descends from, and choosing not to apply the same reuse-prevention period to devices is a defensible position.

## Edge cases

- **Whether device identifiers need the same reuse-prevention period as user identifiers is unresolved in public guidance.** Treating the period as a person-identifier control and not extending it to devices is a defensible reading, given how the underlying language is framed around individuals, groups, and roles rather than hardware.
- **Retaining disabled accounts indefinitely to guarantee an identifier is never reissued raises a separate data-retention question.** A defined, enforced deletion or archival schedule alongside the reuse-prevention period addresses both concerns without conflict.

## Evidence examples

- **Written policy stating the defined identifier reuse period.** [a]
- **Directory export showing disabled or retired accounts retained rather than deleted or immediately reassigned.** [b]
- **System Security Plan language documenting the exception process for the same individual reusing their own prior identifier.** [a][b]
- **Procedure documentation for cloud-only environments describing how the reuse period is enforced without a technical mechanism.** [a][b]

## Related requirements

- **3.5.1** establishes the identifiers this requirement then protects from premature reuse.
- **3.5.6** disables identifiers after inactivity, working alongside this requirement to manage the same identifier's full lifecycle.
- **3.1.1's** authorized-user roster is the record an organization checks before deciding whether a returning individual can receive their prior identifier back.
