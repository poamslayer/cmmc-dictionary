## Requirement

"Provide security awareness training on recognizing and reporting potential indicators of insider threat."

## Objectives

[a] potential indicators associated with insider threats are identified; and
[b] security awareness training on recognizing and reporting potential indicators of insider threat is provided to managers and employees.

## Sufficient

- **A free, government-sourced insider-threat awareness course used as the backbone of the training.** This has been accepted as a complete, ready-made answer to both objectives, since it already identifies indicators and delivers them as awareness content. [a][b]
- **A recurring, no-more-than-annual cadence.** The requirement does not state a frequency in its own text, but reviewers read a recurring cadence as implied, generally treating annual as the outer bound of what counts as periodic. [b]
- **Reusing an existing cleared-personnel insider-threat training obligation and extending it to non-cleared staff who also handle CUI.** Where an organization already runs annual insider-threat training tied to a facility clearance, that same training can serve double duty for this requirement, as long as coverage is not limited to cleared personnel alone. [b]
- **No fixed count of indicators is required.** There is no minimum list length a training program has to hit; a reasonable starter set drawn from a recognized source is enough. [a]

## Insufficient

- **A single training pass treated as a permanent answer.** Delivering the material once and never repeating it tends toward a finding against the organization; the requirement is read as ongoing, not a one-time box to check.
- **A general annual security refresher that only brushes against insider-threat content in passing.** If the topic barely appears, it does not carry the weight objective [b] asks for.

## Over-engineering

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Edge cases

- **Sub-contractor personnel who hold system access but are not directly employed.** Whose responsibility it is to track their insider-threat training, particularly where a facility security function does not consider it part of its own scope, is unresolved; a reasonable position is that anyone with access to the facility or system under the organization's clearance should fall under the same training obligation, but this is not settled.
- **Whether an annual cadence is a hard requirement or only a strong expectation.** A separate government directive sets an explicit annual cadence for government components, and that specific mandate does not extend to contractors by its own terms; reviewers nonetheless tend to expect the same annual rhythm from contractors in practice.
- **Mixed cleared and non-cleared populations.** An organization that already trains its cleared staff under a facility-clearance obligation still needs to bring its non-cleared CUI users into the same training; treating the cleared population as the whole audience misses part of the requirement.

## Evidence examples

- **Completion records from an insider-threat awareness course.** [a][b]
- **A training policy or schedule documenting a recurring, not one-time, cadence.** [b]
- **Coverage records showing both cleared and non-cleared personnel with CUI access received the training.** [b]

## Related requirements

- **3.2.1** is the broader security-risk and policy awareness training this narrower insider-threat topic can be delivered alongside.
- **3.6.1** and **3.6.2**, incident handling and reporting, are the destination for what this training teaches people to recognize and report.
- **3.9.2**, personnel screening, addresses risk before someone is granted access; this requirement addresses ongoing awareness once they already have it.
