## Requirement

"Sanitize or destroy information system media containing Federal Contract Information before disposal or release for reuse."

## Objectives

[a] system media containing FCI is sanitized or destroyed before disposal; and
[b] system media containing FCI is sanitized before it is released for reuse.

## Sufficient

- **The NIST SP 800-88 "Clear" technique for media staying inside the organization**, such as a factory reset or an operating system's built-in "remove everything and clean data" option, provided the specific technique clears every user-addressable storage location on the device. [b]
- **The NIST SP 800-88 "Purge" technique for media leaving the organization**, the approach federal records guidance recommends: a drive-native sanitize command valid for the storage technology in use, or cryptographic erase carried out by deleting the media encryption key on a FIPS-validated encrypted volume. [a][b]
- **Cryptographic erase through encryption key rotation on an already fully encrypted drive.** Current practitioner consensus accepts key rotation as a valid purge technique, provided the organization understands what the specific encryption implementation actually does when the key is destroyed. [a][b]
- **On-site physical destruction (shredding) by a third-party destruction vendor, documented with a certificate of destruction.** Widely accepted as sufficient for both paper and digital media. [a]
- **Third-party paper shredding followed by recycling of the shredded material**, which is treated as compliant destruction under federal records guidance on destroying CUI in paper form. [a]
- **Vendor documentation confirming a specific sanitize action or wipe function meets NIST SP 800-88 Revision 1** for that device. [a][b]
- **A destruction log** tracking the asset identifier, the drive or memory component, and what was done to it. [a][b]

## Insufficient

- **A vendor's own hedge about its "sanitization" feature, with no documentation tying the feature to NIST SP 800-88.** Where a device manufacturer states that full data erasure can only be guaranteed by physically destroying certain internal memory components, that hedge puts the sanitization burden back on the organization; relying on the built-in feature alone does not close it. [a][b]
- **A factory reset applied without verifying what it actually clears.** Whether a given reset technique clears all user-addressable storage per NIST SP 800-88 cannot be assumed; it has to be checked against the specific device and operating system. [b]
- **Deleting an encryption recovery key from an external key-escrow location while leaving the media encryption key itself untouched on the drive.** This is not a cryptographic erase; the actual key material controlling decryption, not just a recovery copy of it, has to be destroyed. [a][b]
- **A sanitization approach whose validity depends entirely on how a specific assessor happens to interpret it, rather than a documented, repeatable technique tied to a published standard.** Practitioner consensus pushes back on building a program around an interpretation rather than a defined method. [a][b]

## Over-engineering

- **Applying destruction standards built for classified national security systems to CUI media.** Practitioner consensus treats this as well beyond what a moderate-confidentiality system needs; NIST SP 800-88 already covers CUI-appropriate methods. [a][b]
- **Requiring a specific particle size for shredded paper.** NIST SP 800-88 Revision 2 removed prescriptive shred-size requirements, and practitioner consensus finds no CMMC-specific shred-size requirement either. [a]
- **Building a full CUI-handling program around the position that encrypted CUI must be treated identically to plaintext CUI in every respect.** Assessors have accepted the narrower disposal-related consequences of this position in practice, but treating it as a blanket mandate for every control is more than the requirement calls for; the outcome depends on the assessor.

## Edge cases

- **Copier or printer internal storage that cannot be removed at the end of a lease.** This needs vendor documentation confirming the device's built-in sanitization meets NIST SP 800-88; without that confirmation, the organization is relying on destruction it cannot independently verify.
- **Multi-step paper destruction, such as a lower-capability shredder followed by burning the shreds.** NIST SP 800-88 allows any method that is verifiable and satisfactory to the organization, so a documented multi-step process can qualify, though it is a less common path likely to draw extra scrutiny during a third-party assessment.
- **A backlog of drives whose prior encryption status is unknown.** A drive later processed with a valid purge command, such as a drive-native sanitize command, is validly sanitized regardless of what encryption state it was in beforehand.
- **Mobile device management "wipe" functionality.** Whether this satisfies the requirement is implementation-specific and needs verification of what the wipe actually does at the storage layer, ideally paired with full-disk encryption already in force before the wipe runs.
- **What standard actually applies when a contract's language differs from NIST SP 800-88.** 32 CFR Part 170 does not itself mandate NIST SP 800-88; where a contract specifies destruction under federal records management guidance instead, that guidance is a valid standard to follow on its own terms.
- **Shredded paper pending final destruction.** Until it is actually destroyed, shredded material still requires the same physical protections given to intact CUI documents.

## Evidence examples

- **Destruction log** recording asset identifier, drive or component, and destruction date. [a][b]
- **Certificate of destruction** from a third-party destruction vendor. [a]
- **Vendor sanitization documentation** confirming a wipe function or sanitize command meets NIST SP 800-88 Revision 1. [a][b]
- **Written sanitization policy** mapping methods to the NIST SP 800-88 Clear, Purge, and Destroy categories. [a][b]
- **Witnessed destruction record** for on-site shredding or drive destruction events. [a]
- **Encryption key rotation record** supporting a cryptographic erase claim. [a][b]

## Related requirements

- **3.8.1** governs how media is protected before it ever reaches disposal; this requirement governs what happens at end of life.
- **3.13.11** supplies the FIPS-validated encryption whose key destruction makes a cryptographic erase valid here.
- **3.8.9** governs backup media while it is in service; once that media is retired, it falls under this requirement.
