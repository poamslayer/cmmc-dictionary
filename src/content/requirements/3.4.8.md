## Requirement

"Apply deny-by-exception (blacklisting) policy to prevent the use of unauthorized software or deny-all, permit-by-exception (whitelisting) policy to allow the execution of authorized software."

## Objectives

[a] a policy specifying whether whitelisting or blacklisting is to be implemented is specified;
[b] the software allowed to execute under whitelisting or denied use under blacklisting is specified; and
[c] whitelisting to allow the execution of authorized software or blacklisting to prevent the use of unauthorized software is implemented as specified.

## Sufficient

- **A written deny list of specific software categories or titles, used as evidence together with an inventory tool confirming none of it is actually installed.** Denied categories cited in practice include end-of-life or end-of-support software, keystroke-logging tools, unapproved antivirus or VPN clients, redundant alternatives to a standardized tool (a second PDF reader or a second CAD package, for example), and games. This pairing of a written list plus inventory confirmation satisfies all three objectives under a blacklisting approach. [a][b][c]
- **A whitelist policy that references an external, maintained list of approved products as its basis**, rather than the organization building its authorized-software list entirely from scratch. [a][b]
- **A list of authorized software combined with a requirement that all installations need administrative privileges**, provided this is paired with an active mechanism that tracks and reviews what gets installed rather than relying on the privilege restriction alone. [a][b][c]

## Insufficient

- **Relying on software inventory as the allowlist by itself, with nothing that pre-approves entries before they can run.** If the inventory is simply whatever happens to be installed, then anything an administrator-level account installs, including something clearly outside a legitimate business purpose, would count as "allowed" purely because it now appears in inventory. An allowlist has to be pre-approved, not simply observed. [b][c]
- **A blacklisting response that lists only a single named product as denied, with nothing else evaluated.** This technically checks the box for objective [b] but is viewed as a minimal, unconvincing answer to a policy that is supposed to reflect the organization's actual software risk, not a token entry. [a][b]

## Over-engineering

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Edge cases

- **Whether admin-required installation plus an always-current software inventory, on its own, satisfies this requirement.** This is not automatically sufficient; it needs to be paired with active tracking of anything added that was not pre-approved and documentation of how an unauthorized installation, once found, was addressed with the person who installed it. A privilege restriction plus a static inventory without that follow-through leaves the requirement only partly met.
- **What happens when someone with elevated privileges installs something outside the approved list anyway.** The expectation is not that this can never happen, but that the organization has a way to detect it (through inventory or endpoint monitoring) and a documented process for what happens next, rather than treating the elevated-privilege requirement alone as sufficient prevention.

## Evidence examples

- **Written deny list** naming denied software categories or titles. [a][b]
- **Software inventory report** (from an endpoint or antivirus tool) confirming denied items are not present. [c]
- **Policy document** stating whether whitelisting or blacklisting is the organization's chosen approach. [a]
- **Allowlist of authorized software**, cross-referenced against installation privilege restrictions. [b][c]
- **Incident or ticket record** documenting an unauthorized installation and how it was resolved. [c]

## Related requirements

- **3.4.1** supplies the software inventory this requirement's allow or deny list is checked against.
- **3.4.9** is the narrower, one-point companion to this requirement: where this requirement governs whether software is allowed to execute at all, 3.4.9 specifically governs and monitors installation by end users.
- **3.4.6** and **3.4.7** share the same underlying judgment about what software, functions, and services are actually needed for business purposes.
