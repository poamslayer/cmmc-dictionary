## Requirement

"Correlate audit record review, analysis, and reporting processes for investigation and response to indications of unlawful, unauthorized, suspicious, or unusual activity."

## Objectives

[a] audit record review, analysis, and reporting processes for investigation and response to indications of unlawful, unauthorized, suspicious, or unusual activity are defined; and
[b] defined audit record review, analysis, and reporting processes are correlated.

## Sufficient

- **Addressing both readings of "correlate" at once, rather than picking just one.** One reading treats correlation as a human workflow: something suspicious appears in the logs, and a person notices and starts an investigation. The other reading treats correlation as a technical capability: pulling logs from every system into one place so they can be cross-referenced automatically. Practitioner consensus and a documented assessment outcome both favor covering both readings together as the safer path. [a][b]
- **A defined chain covering all three stages by name: review, analysis, and reporting, each tied explicitly to investigating and responding to suspicious activity.** Naming all three stages, and showing how each connects to the next, is the accepted structure for objective [a].
- **An automated detection process that produces alerts from predefined queries, feeding directly into the reduction and reporting capability required elsewhere.** Where review and analysis are tightly coupled this way, the reporting step follows naturally as the correlated output. [a][b]
- **A review process that looks at detection alerts, indicators of compromise, file hashes, and behavioral signals, decides whether something is noteworthy, and escalates internally when it is.** [a]
- **Substituting an endpoint detection and response tool's own telemetry for traditional operating-system event logs**, as long as the organization has defined what it is logging and monitoring in a way that lines up with its other logging requirements. Practitioner consensus accepts this as an alternative source, provided the definitions are worked through and matched up rather than assumed. [a]

## Insufficient

- **Paying for an outside monitoring service and reporting zero incidents over an extended period, with nothing to show for the relationship beyond the subscription.** This pattern raises real doubt about whether review is actually connected to analysis and reporting, rather than logs simply accumulating unexamined.
- **Having plenty of logs with no defined link between reviewing them and responding to what is found.** Volume of logging alone does not satisfy this requirement; the missing piece is a documented connection from what the logs show to what the organization does about it.

## Over-engineering

No documented position beyond the Assessment Guide; see confidence.

## Edge cases

- **Whether "correlate" means correlating people-driven processes or correlating machine data.** Both readings are defensible on the requirement's own text, and organizations that address only one risk leaving the other unaddressed if a reviewer holds the other view; the safer path is to build evidence for both. The outcome can still depend on the assessor.
- **Using an endpoint detection tool as the sole source instead of native operating-system logs.** This is accepted where the organization can show the tool covers what it needs, including detecting misuse, recording security-relevant activity, and tying activity back to a directory identity, and where the substitution is explicitly defined rather than left implicit.
- **How often this requirement trips up organizations.** It is commonly failed at assessment, often because logging and incident response exist as separate efforts with no documented bridge between them.

## Evidence examples

- **Procedures addressing investigation of and response to suspicious activity.** [a]
- **A defined chain showing review, analysis, and reporting each named and connected.** [a]
- **Log correlation or centralized monitoring platform configuration**, showing data from multiple sources pulled into one place. [b]
- **A record of a detection-alert review process**, covering how indicators are triaged and escalated. [a][b]
- **Endpoint detection and response telemetry**, documented as the defined alternative to native event logs where used. [a]

## Related requirements

- **3.3.1** through **3.3.3** define, generate, and periodically review the audit records this requirement then correlates.
- **3.3.6** supplies the reduction and reporting capability that makes an automated version of this requirement's review and analysis chain practical.
- **3.6.1** and **3.6.2**, incident handling, are the destination this requirement's correlated review and analysis feed into once something suspicious is confirmed.
